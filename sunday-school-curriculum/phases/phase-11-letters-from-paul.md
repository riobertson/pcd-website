## The Big Idea (in kid words)

Remember Paul from the book of Acts? He started lots of churches in lots of cities. When he couldn't visit them, he wrote them letters! God's Holy Spirit guided Paul's words, so these letters are part of the Bible. Paul's biggest message is this: we can't earn God's love by being good enough — it is a **free gift** called **grace**. When we trust Jesus, God forgives us, makes us part of His family, and helps us live with love, joy, and kindness.

**How this points to Jesus:** Every one of Paul's letters is about Jesus — who He is (God's Son, over everything, Colossians 1:15–20), what He did (died for our sins and rose again, 1 Corinthians 15:3–4), and how He lives in us (Galatians 2:20). Paul said, "For to me, to live is Christ" (Philippians 1:21).

## The Books in Easy Words

> **Big tip for remembering:** Paul's letters are named after **who they were sent to**. Nine are to **churches** (Romans → 2 Thessalonians) and four are to **friends/pastors** (1 Timothy → Philemon). And the middle four have a famous memory trick: **G-E-P-C = "General Electric Power Company"** (Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians).

## Teacher Preparation

### Background for the teacher
Paul (born Saul in Tarsus) was a highly trained Jewish Pharisee who persecuted Christians until Jesus met him on the road to Damascus (Acts 9). He then spent about 30 years starting churches across the Roman Empire. His 13 letters (called **epistles**) were written to real people facing real problems — arguments, false teaching, fear, suffering, and confusion about Jesus' return. They are arranged in the Bible **roughly from longest to shortest**, first the church letters, then the personal letters — not in the order Paul wrote them. (1 Thessalonians and Galatians are probably the earliest; 2 Timothy is the last.)

**Groupings that help:**
- **Church letters (9):** Romans, 1 & 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 & 2 Thessalonians
- **Prison letters (4):** Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, Philemon (+ 2 Timothy, written from a later imprisonment)
- **Pastoral letters (3):** 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy, Titus — instructions for church leaders

Many of these cities appeared in Acts last month: Philippi (Lydia and the jailer, Acts 16), Thessalonica (Acts 17), Corinth (Acts 18), and Ephesus (Acts 19). Connect the dots for the kids — "Remember the jailer? This letter went to his church!"

### Key truths to emphasize
1. **Grace:** salvation is a free gift, received by faith, not earned by works (Ephesians 2:8–9; Romans 6:23).
2. **Grace changes us:** good works don't save us, but they are the result of being saved (Ephesians 2:10; Titus 2:11–14).
3. **Jesus is supreme:** He is God, Creator, and Lord over all (Colossians 1:15–20; Philippians 2:5–11).
4. **The church is one body:** every person matters, every gift is needed (1 Corinthians 12).
5. **Love is the greatest:** (1 Corinthians 13:13).
6. **Hope:** Jesus is coming back, and death is not the end (1 Thessalonians 4:13–18; 1 Corinthians 15).

### Common kid questions (with simple answers)
- **"Why did Paul write letters instead of texting?"** — There were no phones! Letters were carried by friends who walked or sailed for weeks. A letter from Paul was a big deal — the whole church would gather to hear it read.
- **"How are letters to other people God's Word for me?"** — The Holy Spirit guided Paul (2 Timothy 3:16). The same truths that helped those churches help us, because people still need Jesus, still argue, still get scared, and still need hope.
- **"What is grace?"** — Grace is getting a good gift you didn't earn. God gives us forgiveness and His love even though we don't deserve it.
- **"If grace is free, can I just do bad stuff?"** — Paul answered that exact question: "By no means!" (Romans 6:1–2). When you love someone who gave you a huge gift, you want to please them.
- **"Did Paul go to jail because he was bad?"** — No. He was arrested for telling people about Jesus. Some people didn't like his message.
- **"What happened to Paul?"** — The Bible doesn't describe his death, but early Christian writers say he was killed in Rome for his faith, around AD 64–67. Before that, he wrote, "I have finished the race" (2 Timothy 4:7). He is with Jesus now.

## Weekly Lessons

### Week 1 — The Best Gift Ever (Romans & Galatians)

**Scripture:** Romans 3:23; 5:8; 6:23; 8:38–39; 10:9; 16:1–2, 22; Galatians 5:22–23
**Lesson goal:** Kids will understand that salvation is a free gift from God (grace), received by trusting Jesus.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "Earn It or Receive It?"**
*Supplies:* a beautifully wrapped gift box (inside: a card that reads "GRACE — a gift you can't earn"), a few "chores" cards (sweep, jump 10 times, recite ABCs).
1. Show the gift. "Who wants it?" Offer it to a volunteer — but first say, "You have to earn it! Do these 3 chores." Let them start, then stop them.
2. "Wait — if you earn it, is it still a gift? No! It's a paycheck." Hand them the gift for free.
3. Say: "God's love is like this. We can't earn it. Paul called it **grace** — a gift we receive by trusting Jesus."

**Story script:**
> Paul was staying in the city of Corinth. He had never been to Rome, the biggest and most important city in the whole world. But there were Christians in Rome, and Paul loved them. He wanted to send them the clearest explanation he could of the good news about Jesus.
>
> So Paul sat down with a friend named Tertius. Paul talked, and Tertius wrote every word with a pen and ink on a long scroll. It was a very long letter — sixteen chapters!
>
> Here is some of what Paul said. "Everyone has sinned." Sin means doing what's wrong and going our own way instead of God's way. Paul said, "All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." That means nobody is good enough on their own — not kings, not teachers, not even Paul!
>
> "Sin is like a job that pays a terrible paycheck," Paul explained. "The wages of sin is death." That's bad news. But then comes the best news ever: "…but the GIFT of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord!"
>
> How did God give us this gift? Paul wrote, "God showed His love for us like this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us." Jesus didn't wait for us to get good. He came for us while we were still messing up!
>
> And how do we receive the gift? "If you declare with your mouth, 'Jesus is Lord,' and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved."
>
> Paul wanted the Romans to know that once you belong to Jesus, nothing can take you away from His love. "Not death, not life, not angels, not anything in all creation can separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord!"
>
> When the letter was done, a brave woman named Phoebe carried it all the way to Rome — over land and sea. Imagine the church gathering to hear it read out loud for the first time!
>
> In another letter, to the churches in Galatia, Paul explained what happens after we receive God's gift: the Holy Spirit grows "fruit" in our lives — love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. We don't grow good fruit to earn God's love. We grow it because God's love is already living in us!

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Pass the gift box around while singing; whoever holds it when the music stops says, "Jesus is a gift!" Focus phrase: "It is the gift of God."
- **Explorers:** Teach the **Romans Road** with 4 signposts on the wall: 3:23 (everyone sinned), 6:23 (sin pays death, God gives a gift), 5:8 (Jesus died for us), 10:9 (believe and say "Jesus is Lord"). Walk the road together.
- **Trailblazers:** Look up Galatians 5:22–23 and list the nine fruits. Which one is easiest for you? Hardest? Compare with "acts of the flesh" (5:19–21).
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss "justified by faith" (Romans 5:1). What does "justified" mean (declared right/not guilty)? Why couldn't the law save us (Romans 3:20; Galatians 2:16)? Practice walking a younger child through the Romans Road.

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) What is a gift? 2) What gift does God give us? (Jesus / forever life with Him.) 3) Can anything stop God from loving you? (No!)
- *Explorers:* 1) What does "all have sinned" mean? 2) Why is God's gift better than a paycheck? 3) Who carried Paul's letter to Rome?
- *Trailblazers:* 1) Why do people try to earn God's love? 2) What's the difference between doing good to *get* God's love and doing good *because* we have it? 3) Which fruit of the Spirit do you want God to grow in you this month?

### Week 2 — One Body, Big Love, Strong Armor (1 & 2 Corinthians, Ephesians)

**Scripture:** 1 Corinthians 12:12–27; 13:1–13; 2 Corinthians 12:9; Ephesians 6:10–18
**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that every believer is an important part of Christ's body, that love is the greatest, and that God gives us armor to stand strong.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "The Silly Body"**
*Supplies:* a large paper cut-out of a person, extra paper "ears" (lots of them), tape.
1. Tape ten ears all over the paper body. "What if the whole body was an EAR? How would it see? Smell? Walk?"
2. Kids laugh. Say: "Paul wrote to a church where people were saying, 'I'm more important than you!' Paul said, 'You're like a body — every part is needed!'"

**Story script:**
> In the city of Corinth there was a church Paul had started. Paul stayed there a year and a half, teaching about Jesus. But after he left, the church started having problems. People were arguing. Some said, "I follow Paul!" Others said, "I follow Apollos!" Some thought they were more important because they had flashy gifts. Others felt left out.
>
> So Paul wrote them a letter. He said, "You are like a body. A body has many parts — eyes, ears, hands, feet. What if the foot said, 'I'm not a hand, so I don't belong'? It would still be part of the body! What if the whole body were one big eye? How would it hear? God put each part where He wanted it. You are the body of Christ, and each one of you is a part of it."
>
> Then Paul wrote something so beautiful that people still read it at weddings today. "If I can speak every language, even like angels, but don't have love, I'm just a noisy gong! Love is patient. Love is kind. It does not envy. It does not boast. It is not proud. It keeps no record of wrongs. Love never fails!" And Paul ended, "Faith, hope, and love — but the greatest of these is love."
>
> Later, Paul wrote them another letter. He told them about his hard times: he'd been beaten, shipwrecked three times, gone hungry, and even lowered in a basket through a wall to escape. Paul also had something painful he called a "thorn in my flesh." Three times he asked God to take it away. But God said, "My grace is enough for you. My power is made perfect in weakness." Paul learned that when he felt weak, God's strength showed up even more!
>
> Years later, while he was chained to a Roman soldier, Paul wrote to the church in Ephesus. Maybe he looked at that soldier's armor and got an idea. "Put on the full armor of God," he wrote, "so you can stand strong!" There's the **belt of truth**, the **breastplate of righteousness**, **shoes** ready to share the **gospel of peace**, the **shield of faith**, the **helmet of salvation**, and the **sword of the Spirit**, which is the **Word of God**. And don't forget — pray at all times!

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Point to body parts: "Eyes! Ears! Hands! Feet! Every part is important — and YOU are important in God's family!" Put on pretend armor piece by piece with big motions.
- **Explorers:** Read 1 Corinthians 13:4–7, replacing "love" with "Jesus." Then replace it with your own name — "Sam is patient, Sam is kind…" Is it true? Pray for God's help.
- **Trailblazers:** Match each piece of Roman armor to its spiritual meaning; discuss why the sword (God's Word) is the only "attack" piece.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss spiritual gifts (1 Corinthians 12:4–11; Romans 12:6–8). What gifts do you see in each other? Discuss why Paul put the love chapter right in the middle of a discussion about gifts.

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) Is every part of your body important? 2) Are you important in God's family? (Yes!) 3) What is the greatest thing? (Love!)
- *Explorers:* 1) Why was the church in Corinth arguing? 2) What are three things love does or doesn't do? 3) Name three pieces of God's armor.
- *Trailblazers:* 1) What does it mean that the church is "the body of Christ"? 2) Which part of "love is…" is hardest for you? 3) What did God mean by "My power is made perfect in weakness"?
- *Ambassadors:* 1) How can cliques in a youth group be like the problems in Corinth? 2) What "battles" are teens facing, and which armor piece helps most? 3) How can weakness actually be a gift?

### Week 3 — Joy in Chains (Philippians, Colossians, 1 & 2 Thessalonians)

**Scripture:** Philippians 1:12–14, 21; 2:25–30; 4:4–13; Colossians 1:15–20; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18; 5:16–18
**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that real joy comes from Jesus, who is over everything, and that we have hope because He is coming back.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "Joy on the Inside"**
*Supplies:* a can of soda (or bottle of sparkling water), a clear cup, a bag of sugar cubes or a lemon; OR a glow stick.
- Glow stick version: Show an un-cracked glow stick. "It's kind of boring." Turn off the lights and crack it — it glows! "The darker it gets, the brighter it shines. Paul was in a dark place — chained up in prison — but his joy in Jesus shone bright!"

**Story script:**
> Paul was under arrest in Rome. He lived in a house, but a Roman soldier guarded him all day, and Paul was chained. He couldn't travel. He couldn't visit his churches. Some people would be grumpy, sad, or angry. But Paul did something surprising — he wrote a letter about JOY!
>
> The letter was to his friends in Philippi. Remember them? That's where Lydia believed, and where Paul and Silas sang in jail and the jailer's family got saved! The Philippian church loved Paul. They sent a man named Epaphroditus with gifts to help him. But Epaphroditus got so sick he almost died! God had mercy and healed him, and Paul sent him home carrying the letter.
>
> Paul wrote, "Don't worry about me! Because I'm in chains, the whole palace guard has heard about Jesus! And other Christians are getting braver." Paul said, "For me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain." In other words, "If I live, I get to serve Jesus. If I die, I get to be with Jesus. Either way, I win!"
>
> Then Paul wrote, "Rejoice in the Lord always! I'll say it again: Rejoice!" and "Don't worry about anything; instead, pray about everything." And, "I have learned to be content whether I have a lot or a little. I can do all this through Him who gives me strength."
>
> About the same time, Paul wrote to the church in Colossae. Some people there were saying Jesus wasn't enough. Paul wrote, "Jesus is the image of the invisible God! Everything was made through Him and for Him. He holds all things together!" If Jesus holds the whole universe together, He can hold *us* together too.
>
> Years earlier, Paul had written to the church in Thessalonica. Those believers were sad because some of their Christian friends had died. They wondered, "Will we ever see them again?" Paul comforted them: "We don't grieve like people who have no hope. Jesus died and rose again, and one day the Lord Himself will come down from heaven with a loud command and the trumpet call of God. Those who died believing in Jesus will rise, and we will all be with the Lord forever! So encourage each other with these words."
>
> No wonder Paul could write, "Rejoice always, pray continually, give thanks in all circumstances!"

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Sing "I've Got the Joy, Joy, Joy, Joy Down in My Heart." Practice "Rejoice!" as a jump-and-cheer. Short truth: "Jesus makes me happy inside, even on sad days."
- **Explorers:** Play "Glad / Sad / Still Glad": Teacher names a bad situation ("Your ice cream fell!"). Kids say one reason they can still have joy in Jesus.
- **Trailblazers:** Read Philippians 4:6–7 and practice turning worries into prayers. Write a worry on one side of a card and a prayer on the other.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss "content" (Philippians 4:11–13). Is Philippians 4:13 about winning sports games? (In context it's about being content in need or plenty.) Discuss Colossians 1:15–20 as one of the earliest Christian statements that Jesus is God.

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) Where was Paul when he wrote about joy? (In chains!) 2) Who gives us joy? (Jesus.) 3) Who is coming back one day? (Jesus!)
- *Explorers:* 1) How did Paul's chains actually help spread the gospel? 2) What does Philippians 4:6 tell us to do instead of worrying? 3) Why were the Thessalonians sad, and what did Paul tell them?
- *Trailblazers:* 1) What's the difference between happiness and joy? 2) What does "He holds all things together" mean for your life? 3) How can you "give thanks in all circumstances" — even bad ones?
- *Ambassadors:* 1) What does Philippians 4:13 really mean in context? 2) How does hope in Jesus' return change the way Christians handle grief? 3) Paul says "to die is gain." How can that be true without being morbid?

### Week 4 — Letters to Friends (1 & 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon)

**Scripture:** 1 Timothy 4:12; 2 Timothy 1:5; 3:14–17; 4:6–8; Titus 3:4–7; Philemon 1–25
**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that God uses young people and that forgiveness turns enemies into family.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "The Debt Note"**
*Supplies:* a paper "bill" that reads "OWES: $1,000,000," a red marker, an envelope.
1. Hand a volunteer the bill. "Oh no — you owe a million dollars! Can you pay it?" (No!)
2. Take the red marker and write across it: "CHARGE IT TO ME. — Paul." "Today we'll read a letter where Paul says, 'If he owes you anything, charge it to me.' That's what Jesus does for us!"

**Story script:**
> Paul had a lot of friends, and some of his letters were written just to them.
>
> One friend was **Timothy**. Timothy grew up in a town called Lystra. His grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice taught him the Scriptures from the time he was a little boy. When Paul visited, Timothy believed in Jesus and began traveling with Paul. Paul called him "my true son in the faith."
>
> When Timothy became a pastor in Ephesus, he was pretty young. Maybe some people said, "You're just a kid! What do you know?" So Paul wrote, "Don't let anyone look down on you because you are young, but set an example for the believers in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, and in purity." God uses young people!
>
> Later, Paul was in prison again, and this time he knew he would probably die soon. He wrote one last letter to Timothy: "Keep going in what you learned. All Scripture is God-breathed. Preach the Word! I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith. Now there is a crown waiting for me." Paul also wrote, "Come see me soon. Bring my coat and my scrolls. Only Luke is with me."
>
> Another friend was **Titus**, who was helping churches on the island of Crete. Paul told him, "Remind people to be kind and ready to do good — because God our Savior was kind to us. He saved us, not because of the good things we did, but because of His mercy."
>
> And then there's the shortest letter: **Philemon**. Philemon was a rich Christian who had a church meeting in his house. He also had a slave named Onesimus. One day, Onesimus ran away — and he may have taken something too. He ran all the way to the big city of Rome.
>
> Guess who Onesimus met there? Paul! Paul told him about Jesus, and Onesimus became a Christian. His name meant "useful," and now he really was useful — he helped Paul a lot.
>
> But Onesimus needed to make things right. So Paul sent him back home with a letter for Philemon: "I'm sending him back to you — not as a slave anymore, but as a dear brother. Welcome him like you would welcome me. If he owes you anything, charge it to me. I'll pay it back!"
>
> Can you imagine Philemon reading that letter with Onesimus standing right in front of him? That's the power of the gospel: Jesus forgives us and makes us brothers and sisters — and He paid what we owed.

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Act out the Philemon story with two puppets: running away, saying sorry, getting a hug. "God forgives us, and we forgive others."
- **Explorers:** Brainstorm ways kids can "set an example" in each area: speech, conduct (actions), love, faith, purity. Write them on a hand outline (five fingers = five areas).
- **Trailblazers:** Read all of Philemon (it's short — 25 verses). Find where Paul is gentle, where he is persuasive, and where he offers to pay. Compare Philemon 18 with 2 Corinthians 5:21.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss 2 Timothy 3:16–17 — what does "God-breathed" mean for how we treat the Bible? Write a "Paul-style" letter of encouragement to a younger kid in the class.

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) Who taught Timothy about God when he was little? (His grandma and mom.) 2) Can God use kids? (Yes!) 3) What did Paul ask Philemon to do? (Forgive / welcome Onesimus.)
- *Explorers:* 1) What five ways did Paul say Timothy could be an example? 2) Why did Onesimus have to go back? 3) How is Paul saying "charge it to me" like what Jesus did?
- *Trailblazers:* 1) Why do you think Paul's last letter talks so much about God's Word? 2) What does it mean to "finish the race"? 3) Who is someone you need to forgive or ask forgiveness from?
- *Ambassadors:* 1) How can a teenager be a leader in church now? 2) What would it have cost Philemon to obey Paul? 3) How should the gospel change the way we treat people with less power or status?

## Games

### 🎮 1. Romans Road Race
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers (Little Lambs with buddies)
- **Supplies:** Four large "road signs" (3:23, 6:23, 5:8, 10:9) posted around the room, tape "road" lines, paper "cars" or just running feet
- **Setup:** Place signs in a loop. At each sign, a card says what the verse means in kid words.
- **How to play:** Teams race from sign to sign. At each sign, they must say the meaning ("Everyone sinned!") before running on. At the finish, they shout the memory verse.
- **How to win:** First team to complete the road and say Ephesians 2:8 together.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* just shout one word at each sign (Sin! Gift! Jesus! Believe!). *Older:* must quote each verse from memory.
- **Learning goal:** The gospel in four steps; grace.

### 🎮 2. Armor Up Relay
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Supplies:** Two sets of "armor": a belt (rope or old belt), a breastplate (paper grocery bag with arm holes), shoes (big adult slippers), a shield (paper plate or cardboard), a helmet (plastic bowl or hat), a sword (rolled-up newspaper or Bible). Labels on each.
- **Setup:** Armor piles at the far end of the room for each team.
- **How to play:** Runner 1 runs to the pile, puts on the belt, says "Belt of truth!" and runs back. Runner 2 grabs the breastplate, and so on, until one teammate is fully dressed. That "soldier" must then say Ephesians 6:11 ("Put on the full armor of God…") or just "I'm ready!"
- **How to win:** First fully armored soldier wins.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* teacher helps put armor on. *Older:* each runner must also name a real-life way to use that piece.
- **Learning goal:** The six pieces of God's armor (Ephesians 6).

### 🎮 3. Body Parts Scramble
- **Best for:** Little Lambs & Explorers
- **Supplies:** None (or cards with body parts drawn on them)
- **How to play:** Teacher calls out two body parts: "Elbow to knee!" Kids find a partner and touch those parts together. When the teacher shouts "ONE BODY!" everyone joins in one giant group hug/huddle and shouts, "We are the body of Christ!" Then the teacher calls a Paul letter, and kids shout the next one.
- **How to win:** Everyone wins when the whole class is one body!
- **Adaptations:** *Older:* add "Name a spiritual gift!" challenges.
- **Learning goal:** 1 Corinthians 12 — we need each other.

### 🎮 4. Joy Jump
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Supplies:** Open space, a list of situations
- **How to play:** Teacher reads a situation. If it's a *happy* situation, kids jump and shout "Rejoice!" If it's a *hard* situation, kids crouch — then the teacher says "But Jesus is still with me!" and everyone springs up and shouts "Rejoice ANYWAY!" Between rounds, call a Paul letter; kids answer "Church!" or "Friend!"
- **How to win:** This is a cooperative game; the goal is for everyone to jump together.
- **Adaptations:** *Older:* kids must say a verse from Philippians after each "Rejoice anyway!"
- **Learning goal:** Joy in every circumstance (Philippians 4:4; 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18).

### 🎮 5. Mail Carrier Dash
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers, Ambassadors
- **Supplies:** 13 envelopes, each labeled with one letter of Paul (Romans → Philemon), 9 "church" mailboxes (shoeboxes labeled Rome, Corinth, Corinth, Galatia, Ephesus, Philippi, Colossae, Thessalonica, Thessalonica) and 4 "friend" mailboxes (Timothy, Timothy, Titus, Philemon)
- **Setup:** Spread mailboxes around the room. Put envelopes in a central "post office."
- **How to play:** Teams of mail carriers race to deliver each envelope to the correct mailbox. Then they must line up all 13 envelopes in Bible order.
- **How to win:** Fastest correct delivery + correct order.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* only deliver 4 (Romans, Ephesians, Philippians, Philemon) with picture clues. *Older:* add a third step — say one key verse from each letter.
- **Learning goal:** Who each letter was written to; order of the books.

### 🎮 6. Letter Sort Showdown (Review Sunday)
- **Best for:** Mixed-age teams
- **Supplies:** Two sets of all 57 book cards, color-coded by section
- **How to play:** Teams race to put all 57 books (or just the New Testament 18 for younger teams) in order. Midway, the teacher shouts "Section Shout!" and teams must stop and do the Section Shout for a section they've completed.
- **How to win:** First complete and correct line wins; bonus points for each correct "In one word" given for a Paul letter.
- **Learning goal:** Cumulative review.

### 🧠 Big Questions for Ambassadors (apologetics & worldview)
1. **"Didn't Paul invent Christianity and change Jesus' simple message?"**
   *Suggested answer:* Paul consistently points back to what he *received* from others (1 Corinthians 15:3 — "what I received I passed on to you"), including the early creed about Jesus' death, burial, resurrection, and appearances. Paul met with Peter and James (Galatians 1:18–19; 2:9), who agreed with his message. Jesus Himself taught grace (Luke 18:9–14), His death as a ransom (Mark 10:45), and the inclusion of the nations (Matthew 28:19). Paul explains Jesus — he doesn't replace Him.
2. **"If we're saved by grace and not by works, doesn't that let people do whatever they want?"**
   *Suggested answer:* Paul anticipated this objection: "Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase? By no means!" (Romans 6:1–2). Grace doesn't just forgive; it transforms (Titus 2:11–12). A person who truly receives grace is given a new heart and the Holy Spirit (Ephesians 2:10; Galatians 5:22–25). Good works are the fruit of salvation, not the root.
3. **"Why didn't Paul just say slavery was wrong?"**
   *Suggested answer:* Paul wrote to small, powerless house churches inside an empire where slavery was everywhere, and a call to violent revolt would likely have ended in mass executions. But Paul planted truths that undermine slavery: he condemned "slave traders" (1 Timothy 1:10), taught that in Christ "there is neither slave nor free" (Galatians 3:28), encouraged slaves to gain freedom if they could (1 Corinthians 7:21), told masters they too have a Master in heaven and must treat slaves justly (Colossians 4:1), and asked Philemon to receive Onesimus "no longer as a slave… but as a dear brother" (Philemon 16). Those seeds grew — many Christian abolitionists like William Wilberforce and Frederick Douglass appealed to the Bible's teaching that every person bears God's image. It's honest to admit that some people have twisted the Bible to defend slavery; the Bible's own story points toward freedom and dignity.
