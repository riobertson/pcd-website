## The Big Idea (in kid words)

God's people were slaves in Egypt, and God **set them free**! He did amazing miracles, opened a path through the sea, and fed them with bread from heaven. Then God gave them **good rules** (the Ten Commandments), not to spoil their fun, but to show them how to love God and love people. God even came to **live with them** in a special tent. Even when they grumbled and disobeyed, God stayed faithful. Before they went into the Promised Land, Moses told them: **"Love the LORD your God with all your heart!"**

**How this points to Jesus:** On the first Passover, the blood of a lamb protected God's people. Jesus is "our Passover lamb" (1 Corinthians 5:7), and His blood saves us. God brought His people out of slavery to Egypt; Jesus frees us from slavery to sin (John 8:36). The manna was bread from heaven; Jesus said, "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35). The tabernacle was God living with His people; Jesus "made his dwelling among us" (John 1:14). The bronze snake on a pole saved people who looked at it; Jesus was lifted up on the cross so that everyone who believes in Him may have eternal life (John 3:14–15). Moses promised that God would raise up "a prophet like me" (Deuteronomy 18:15), and that prophet is Jesus (Acts 3:22). Nobody can keep God's rules perfectly, except Jesus, who kept them for us.

## Teacher Preparation

### Background for the teacher (plain adult language)

These four books continue straight on from Genesis. At the end of Genesis, Jacob's family (about 70 people) lives in Egypt. About 400 years later (Exodus 12:40 says 430 years), they've grown into a large nation, but a new Pharaoh has made them slaves.

- **Exodus** has two halves: **rescue** (chapters 1–18: Moses, plagues, Passover, Red Sea, wilderness) and **relationship** (19–40: God makes a covenant at Mount Sinai, gives the law, and comes to dwell in the tabernacle). The order matters: God rescues *first*, then gives the law. The law is not "how to earn God's love," it's "how to live as people God has already saved." Notice the first words of the Ten Commandments: "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt..." (Exodus 20:2).
- **Leviticus** answers a question raised at the end of Exodus: Moses couldn't even enter the tabernacle because of God's glory (Exodus 40:35). How can a holy God live among sinful people? Leviticus gives the answer through sacrifices (sin is serious; it costs a life, Leviticus 17:11), priests (go-betweens), and holiness in daily life. Chapter 16 (the Day of Atonement) is the heart of the book. Hebrews 9–10 explains how Jesus fulfills all of this.
- **Numbers** covers about 40 years in the wilderness. The first generation refused to enter the land after the spies' report (13–14), so they wandered until a new generation grew up. Themes: grumbling and unbelief versus God's patient faithfulness. Even Moses failed (20:12).
- **Deuteronomy** is Moses' farewell sermons on the plains of Moab, just before Israel enters Canaan. It calls a new generation to remember, love and obey. It's quoted often in the New Testament, especially by Jesus.

**The Ten Commandments (kid version).** *Note: Christian traditions number them slightly differently; this is the common Protestant numbering.*
1. Love God most: have no other gods. 2. Don't make idols to worship. 3. Use God's name with respect. 4. Keep the Sabbath day holy (rest and worship). 5. Honor your father and mother. 6. Don't murder. 7. Keep marriage promises (don't commit adultery). 8. Don't steal. 9. Don't lie about others. 10. Don't covet (be content with what you have).

Jesus summed them up as **love God** (1–4) and **love your neighbor** (5–10) (Matthew 22:37–40, quoting Deuteronomy 6:5 and Leviticus 19:18).

**The ten plagues (in order):** (1) water to blood, (2) frogs, (3) gnats, (4) flies, (5) livestock die, (6) boils, (7) hail, (8) locusts, (9) darkness, (10) death of the firstborn. Each showed God's power over Egypt's false gods (Exodus 12:12).

### Key truths to emphasize
1. **God hears and rescues** His people (Exodus 2:24; 3:7).
2. **God's rules are good gifts** that show us how to love Him and others (Deuteronomy 10:13, "for your own good").
3. **God is holy**, and sin is serious, but God provides a way to come close (Leviticus 16–17).
4. **Grumbling and unbelief** are dangerous; trusting God brings blessing (Numbers 14).
5. **Love God with everything** and remember what He's done (Deuteronomy 6:4–9).
6. **Jesus is the true Passover Lamb, Bread of Life, perfect Sacrifice and greater Prophet.**

### Timeline and map notes
- **Timeline:** Joseph's family in Egypt → about 400 years → slavery → Moses born → Moses flees to Midian (age 40, Acts 7:23) → burning bush → Exodus from Egypt (Moses about 80, Exodus 7:7). Traditional dating puts the exodus around 1446 BC (some scholars prefer around 1260 BC) → Mount Sinai (about 1 year) → 40 years wandering → Moses dies at 120 (Deuteronomy 34:7).
- **Map:** Egypt (Goshen in the Nile delta) → across the "Red Sea" (Hebrew *Yam Suph*, "Sea of Reeds"; exact location debated) → the Sinai wilderness → Mount Sinai (also called Horeb) → Kadesh Barnea (where the spies were sent from) → around Edom → the plains of Moab across the Jordan River from Jericho → Mount Nebo. Add these to your class timeline rope and wall map.

### Common kid questions (with simple answers)
- **"Why did God make Pharaoh's heart hard?"** Pharaoh hardened his own heart first, again and again (Exodus 8:15, 32). Eventually God confirmed the choice Pharaoh kept making. God gave him many chances.
- **"Do we still have to keep all the rules in Leviticus?"** Jesus fulfilled the sacrifices and the special worship rules (Hebrews 10:1, 10). But the heart of God's law, loving God and loving others, is still how we live, with the Holy Spirit's help.
- **"Why did people have to kill animals?"** To show that sin is really serious and causes death. Those sacrifices were like arrows pointing to Jesus, the final sacrifice. Now no more are needed!
- **"Why couldn't Moses go into the Promised Land?"** Moses disobeyed God by striking the rock instead of speaking to it, and he took credit (Numbers 20:8–12). God still let him see the land, and later Moses stood in the Promised Land with Jesus at the Transfiguration (Matthew 17:3)!
- **"Was the manna real food?"** Yes! It tasted like wafers made with honey (Exodus 16:31). God fed them every day for 40 years.
- **"Why did the people make a golden calf?"** They got impatient waiting for Moses and wanted a god they could see. People still do that with money, phones or popularity.
- **"Can a donkey really talk?"** God made the donkey speak (Numbers 22:28). The God who made donkeys can do that!
- **"Are the Ten Commandments still for us?"** Yes. They show us God's good way and show us we need Jesus because we break them. Jesus helps us obey from the heart.

## Weekly Lessons

### Week 1: God Sets His People Free 🔥🌊

**Scripture:** Exodus 1:8–2:10; 3:1–15; 7–12 (summary); 12:1–13; 14:10–31

**Lesson goal:** Kids will know that God saw His people's suffering, called Moses, defeated Pharaoh with ten plagues, saved His people through the Passover lamb, and opened the Red Sea, and that Jesus is our Passover Lamb who sets us free from sin.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "Chains Off!"**
- *Supplies:* A paper chain (or yarn loops) loosely around a volunteer's wrists; a red paper door frame taped around the classroom door.
- *Do:* "Imagine you had to work all day, every day, and never got to go home or play. That's what it was like for God's people in Egypt. They were slaves." Have the class shout "Let my people go!" and the volunteer breaks the paper chain. "God did that for His people, and Jesus does that for us when it comes to sin!"

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> Remember Joseph's family that moved to Egypt? Many, many years later, the family had grown HUGE, like the stars God promised Abraham! But a new Pharaoh (king) was afraid of them. He made them **slaves**, making bricks in the hot sun. He even ordered that the baby boys be thrown into the Nile River.
>
> One mom hid her baby boy. When he got too big to hide, she made a basket, covered it with tar so it would float, and set it in the tall grass by the river. His big sister **Miriam** watched. Pharaoh's daughter found the baby, felt sorry for him, and adopted him! She named him **Moses**, which sounds like "drawn out of the water."
>
> Moses grew up in the palace, but one day he ran away to the desert and became a shepherd. When he was 80 years old, he saw a **bush on fire, but it didn't burn up!** God spoke from the bush: "Moses! Take off your sandals, because this is holy ground. I have seen my people's misery. I've heard them crying. Go to Pharaoh and bring my people out!"
>
> Moses said, "Who am I? And who should I say sent me?" God said, "**I AM WHO I AM.** Tell them I AM sent you." God promised to go with Moses, and Moses' brother **Aaron** went too.
>
> Moses told Pharaoh, "**Let my people go!**" Pharaoh said NO. So God sent **ten plagues**: the river turned to blood, then came frogs, gnats, flies, sick animals, boils, hail, locusts and darkness. Every time, Pharaoh said no.
>
> Then God told His people to get ready for the tenth and last plague. Each family took a perfect **lamb** and put its blood on the sides and top of their doorframe. That night, death came to the oldest son in every house in Egypt, but **passed over** every house with the lamb's blood. That's why it's called **Passover**. Finally, Pharaoh said, "GO!"
>
> God led His people with a tall cloud by day and fire by night. But Pharaoh changed his mind and chased them with chariots! The people were trapped at the **Red Sea**. Moses said, "Don't be afraid! The LORD will fight for you." Moses raised his staff, and God sent a strong wind that **split the sea**, with walls of water on both sides! God's people walked through on **dry ground**. When Pharaoh's army followed, the waters came back down.
>
> On the other side, Moses and Miriam sang and danced: "The LORD is my strength and my song!"
>
> Jesus is our Passover Lamb. When we trust Him, His blood covers our sin and God's judgment passes over us. Jesus sets us free!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Act it out: crouch to hide baby Moses, say "Let my people go!", count plagues on fingers, then walk between two teachers holding up blue sheets ("walls of water"). Big takeaway: "God rescues!"
- **Explorers:** Have them list the 10 plagues in order with motions (see the Plague Pop-Up game). Read Exodus 12:13 in their Bibles: "When I see the blood, I will pass over you."
- **Trailblazers:** Discuss God's name "I AM" (Exodus 3:14) and Jesus' "I am" statements in John. Map the route out of Egypt. Ask why God rescued *before* giving the law.
- **Ambassadors:** Study 1 Corinthians 5:7 and John 1:29 alongside Exodus 12. Discuss Pharaoh's hardened heart and what it teaches about repeatedly saying no to God.

**💬 Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) Where did Moses' mom put baby Moses? (In a basket) (2) What did God talk from? (A burning bush) (3) What did God do to the sea? (Split it / opened a path)
- *Explorers:* (1) What did the families put on their doors? Why? (2) How many plagues were there? Name three. (3) When the people were trapped, what did Moses say? ("Don't be afraid; the LORD will fight for you.")
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Moses felt like he wasn't good enough ("Who am I?"). How did God answer? (2) Why do you think Pharaoh kept saying no? (3) How is the Passover lamb like Jesus?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) What does the name "I AM" tell us about God? (2) The plagues judged Egypt's false gods (12:12). What "gods" do people trust in today? (3) In what ways can someone be "free" on the outside but still a slave on the inside (John 8:34–36)?

### Week 2: God's Good Rules and God's Tent ⛰️📜

**Scripture:** Exodus 16:1–31; 17:1–7; 19:16–20:17; 32:1–20; 40:34–38

**Lesson goal:** Kids will know that God provided food and water in the wilderness, gave the Ten Commandments as good gifts, forgave His people after the golden calf, and came to live among them in the tabernacle.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "Rules for Our Good"**
- *Supplies:* A board game with its rule sheet, or a picture of a playground with a fence near a busy road.
- *Do:* "What would happen if we played this game with no rules?" (Chaos! Fights!) "What's the fence by the road for?" (To keep kids safe.) "God's rules are like the fence. They're not there to stop our fun. They're there because God loves us and wants us safe and happy with Him."

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> After crossing the Red Sea, God's people walked into the desert. Soon they started **grumbling**: "We're hungry! We had more food in Egypt!" But God was kind. He said, "I will rain down bread from heaven." The next morning the ground was covered with thin white flakes. The people said, "**Manna?**" which means "What is it?" It tasted like wafers made with honey. Every morning God sent manna, enough for each day, and on the sixth day enough for two days so they could rest on the Sabbath. God also sent quail (birds) for meat and water out of a rock!
>
> Then they came to **Mount Sinai**. The mountain shook! There was thunder, lightning, smoke, and a trumpet blast that got louder and louder. God came down on the mountain and spoke to His people. God said, "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt." *(I rescued you, so here's how to live as My people.)*
>
> Then God gave **Ten Commandments**:
> 1. Love God most. 2. Don't make idols. 3. Respect God's name. 4. Keep the Sabbath holy. 5. Honor your parents. 6. Don't murder. 7. Keep marriage promises. 8. Don't steal. 9. Don't lie. 10. Don't want what belongs to others.
>
> God wrote them on **two stone tablets**. Moses stayed on the mountain with God for 40 days and 40 nights.
>
> But down at the bottom, the people got tired of waiting. They asked Aaron to make them a god. Aaron melted their gold earrings and made a **golden calf**, and they bowed down to it! When Moses came down and saw it, he was so upset that he threw down the tablets and they broke. The people had broken God's first two rules already.
>
> But Moses prayed for the people, and God **forgave** them. God gave the commandments on new tablets. God told Moses, "I am compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in love."
>
> Then God said, "Build Me a special tent, a **tabernacle**, so I can live among you." Everyone brought gifts. When it was finished, a cloud covered the tent and **God's glory filled it**. God was living right in the middle of His people!
>
> Years later, God came to live with people in an even closer way. Jesus was born and "made his dwelling among us" (John 1:14). And nobody kept God's rules perfectly, except Jesus. He kept them for us and died for every time we've broken them.

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Teach five easy rule motions: love God (point up), tell the truth (finger to lips, then open hand), don't take (hands behind back), honor parents (bow), rest (pillow hands). Toss white cotton-ball "manna."
- **Explorers:** Learn the Ten Commandments with finger numbers. Talk about which ones are about loving God (1–4) and which are about loving others (5–10).
- **Trailblazers:** Discuss why the law came *after* rescue. Compare the golden calf to modern idols. Look at Exodus 34:6–7, God's description of Himself.
- **Ambassadors:** Read Matthew 5:21–28 (Jesus on the heart behind the commands) and Galatians 3:24 (the law leads us to Christ). Discuss: if no one can keep the law perfectly, why did God give it?

**💬 Discussion questions:**

### Week 3: Holy God, Wandering People 🐐🍇🐍

**Scripture:** Leviticus 16:1–22 (Day of Atonement); 19:2, 18; Numbers 13:1–14:35; 21:4–9

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that God is holy and provided a way for sins to be forgiven (sacrifice and the Day of Atonement), and that the people's lack of trust led to 40 years of wandering while Joshua and Caleb trusted God, and that Jesus is our perfect sacrifice, lifted up like the bronze snake.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "Clean Hands"**
- *Supplies:* A clear glass of clean water and a few drops of iodine (from a pharmacy), plus a crushed vitamin C tablet. Vitamin C turns iodine-tinted water clear again. *(Do a test run first, and keep the iodine away from little hands.)* Simpler option: a white T-shirt with a ketchup stain and a stain-remover pen.
- *Do:* "God is holy. That means He is perfectly pure, like this clean water. Our sin is like this iodine. Just a few drops change everything!" Add the iodine. "Can the water get clean by itself? No. But God provides a way." (Stir in the vitamin C until the water clears, or use the stain-remover.) "In Leviticus, God gave sacrifices to cover sin. Now Jesus makes us clean all the way!"

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> God's people were camped around the tabernacle, God's special tent. But there was a problem. God is **holy**, perfectly good, pure and bright, and people are sinful. How could they come near God?
>
> In the book of **Leviticus**, God gave the answer. He chose Aaron and his sons to be **priests**, helpers who went between God and the people. When people sinned, they brought an animal, like a lamb or a goat, as a **sacrifice**. It showed that sin is very serious, but God would forgive.
>
> Once a year was a super-important day called the **Day of Atonement**. The high priest took two goats. One goat was offered to God for the people's sins. Then the high priest put his hands on the head of the second goat and confessed all the people's sins over it. That goat, the **scapegoat**, was sent far away into the wilderness, as if carrying their sins away! God also told His people, "**Be holy, because I am holy**," and "**Love your neighbor as yourself.**"
>
> Then it was time to travel to the Promised Land. In the book of **Numbers**, Moses sent **12 spies** to check out the land of Canaan. They came back carrying a bunch of grapes so big that two men had to carry it on a pole! "The land is amazing!" they said. "But... the people there are giants. We felt like grasshoppers!" Ten spies said, "We can't do it."
>
> But two spies, **Joshua** and **Caleb**, said, "The land is very good! If the LORD is pleased with us, He will lead us in. Don't be afraid!" Sadly, the people listened to the ten scared spies. They cried and even wanted to go back to Egypt.
>
> Because they didn't trust God, God said that grown-up generation would wander in the wilderness for **40 years**, until their children were ready. Only Joshua and Caleb from that generation would enter the land.
>
> Even in the wilderness, God took care of them. But once, the people grumbled again and poisonous snakes came into the camp. People were getting bitten! God told Moses, "Make a snake out of bronze and put it up on a pole. Anyone who is bitten can **look at it and live**." And everyone who looked, lived!
>
> Jesus said, "Just as Moses lifted up the snake in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up, that everyone who believes may have eternal life" (John 3:14–15). When we look to Jesus on the cross and trust Him, we are saved. Jesus is our perfect sacrifice; He carries our sins away forever!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Focus on two pictures: big grapes (Joshua and Caleb were brave: "God is with us!") and the snake on the pole ("Look and live!"). Have them practice brave voices: "Don't be afraid! God is with us!"
- **Explorers:** Act out the spy report: 10 kids crouch like grasshoppers; 2 stand tall. Read Numbers 14:9 together. Explain the scapegoat with a "sins" sticky-note on a toy goat that gets carried out of the room.
- **Trailblazers:** Compare the Day of Atonement with Hebrews 9:11–14 and 10:11–14. Discuss: why did fear spread so quickly among the people? Look at the 40 days of spying versus the 40 years of wandering (Numbers 14:34).
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss holiness: not just "rules" but being set apart for God in every area. Read Leviticus 19 and notice how practical it is (care for the poor, honesty, the deaf and blind, foreigners). Discuss trusting God when "giants" are real.

**💬 Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) What fruit did the spies bring back? (Grapes!) (2) Which two spies were brave? (Joshua and Caleb) (3) What did people do to get better from snake bites? (Look at the snake on the pole)
- *Explorers:* (1) What does "holy" mean? (2) Why were 10 spies afraid? (3) How is the snake on the pole like Jesus?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) What was the scapegoat a picture of? (2) What "giants" (big scary problems) do kids face today? What would Joshua and Caleb say? (3) Why did God make the people wait 40 years?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Leviticus 19:18 says "love your neighbor as yourself." Why is it significant that this command sits in a book full of holiness laws? (2) How does Hebrews describe Jesus as better than the high priests? (3) Grumbling is a big theme in Numbers. Why does God take grumbling so seriously?

### Week 4: Remember and Love God ❤️

**Scripture:** Deuteronomy 6:4–9; 8:2–3; 30:19–20; 31:6–8; 34:1–12

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn Moses' final message: love God with all your heart, remember what He has done, teach it to your children, and choose life, and that God was faithful to Moses to the very end.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "String on Your Finger"**
- *Supplies:* Yarn pieces to tie around each child's finger; sticky notes.
- *Do:* "What do people do to remember important things?" (Sticky notes, alarms, a string on a finger.) Tie a yarn on each finger. "Before Moses died, he said: Don't forget God! Remember all He did! Write His words on your doorframes, talk about them at home, when you walk and when you go to bed."

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> After 40 years in the wilderness, God's people were finally camped right next to the Promised Land, just across the Jordan River. The old, grumbling generation was gone. Their children were grown up now, ready to go in.
>
> Moses was 120 years old. He knew he wouldn't go into the land with them. So he gathered everyone together and gave them some of the most important speeches in the whole Bible. That's the book of **Deuteronomy**, which means "second law," because Moses repeated God's law to the new generation.
>
> Moses said: "**Remember!** Remember how God brought you out of Egypt. Remember how He fed you with manna for 40 years. Your clothes didn't even wear out! God was teaching you that people don't live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD."
>
> Then Moses said the most important words of all: "**Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. Love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength.**" God's people call this the **Shema** (shuh-MAH), which means "Hear!"
>
> "Keep God's words in your hearts," Moses said. "Teach them to your children. Talk about them when you sit at home, when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up. Write them on the doorframes of your houses!"
>
> Moses told them, "I have set before you life and death. Now **choose life**, so that you and your children may live, and that you may love the LORD your God and listen to Him."
>
> Then Moses called **Joshua** in front of everyone. "Be strong and courageous! The LORD Himself goes before you and will be with you. He will **never leave you**." Joshua would be the new leader.
>
> Finally, God took Moses up to the top of **Mount Nebo**. From there, God showed him the whole Promised Land, the land He had promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Then Moses died, and God Himself buried him. The Bible says there was never another prophet like Moses, who knew the LORD face to face.
>
> But God had promised a greater Prophet who would come someday. When Jesus was asked, "What is the greatest commandment?" He answered with Moses' words: "Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind." Jesus loved God perfectly, for us, and He helps us love God too.

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Teach "Love the LORD your God" with motions: point up (LORD), hands on heart (love), arms around self (all of me!). Practice "Remember!" by tapping heads.
- **Explorers:** Memorize the whole Shema (Deuteronomy 6:4–5). Make a list of "things God has done for my family" to remember.
- **Trailblazers:** Look up Jesus' three temptation answers in Matthew 4 and find each in Deuteronomy (8:3; 6:16; 6:13). Discuss what it means to "choose life."
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss "heart, soul, strength" (all of you). How do Christians "write God's words on doorframes" today? Plan how they will help pass faith to younger kids (they're doing it this year!).

**💬 Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) Who should we love most? (God!) (2) How old was Moses? (120, very old!) (3) Who was the new leader after Moses? (Joshua)
- *Explorers:* (1) What does "Deuteronomy" mean? (2) Where did Moses tell people to talk about God's words? (3) What's one thing God has done for you that you want to remember?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Why did Moses repeat the law to the new generation? (2) Why did Jesus use Deuteronomy against the devil? What does that teach us? (3) What does it look like to love God with your strength?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Deuteronomy says God's commands are "not too difficult" and "very near you" (30:11–14). Paul applies this to the gospel in Romans 10:6–10. What's the connection? (2) What would a home look like where God's words are talked about "when you walk along the road"? (3) Moses didn't get to enter the land, but he was faithful. How do you define success?

**Ambassadors vocab with definitions:**
- **Pentateuch** (PEN-tuh-took): Greek for "five scrolls"; the first five books of the Bible (also called the Torah, Hebrew for "instruction" or "law").
- **Atonement:** making things right between God and people by dealing with sin; the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16) points to Jesus' death.
- **Covenant:** a binding promise-relationship; at Sinai, God made a covenant with Israel (Exodus 19:5–6; 24:7–8).
- **Sacrifice:** an offering given to God; in Leviticus, an animal's life given in place of the sinner's (17:11). Jesus is the final sacrifice (Hebrews 10:12).
- **Shema** (shuh-MAH): Hebrew for "Hear!"; Deuteronomy 6:4–5, the central confession of Israel's faith.

## Games 🎮

*(More games and variations: `../00-start-here/game-library.md`)*

### 1. Red Sea Crossing
- **Best for:** All ages.
- **Supplies:** Two long blue sheets, tablecloths or streamers (the "walls of water"); two adult or Ambassador helpers; a "Pharaoh" (a teacher wearing a paper crown).
- **Setup:** "Egypt" at one end of the room, "dry land" at the other, the sea in the middle. Helpers hold the sheets low across the middle (the sea is "closed").
- **How to play:** Ask a review question. When it's answered correctly, the helpers lift the sheets up to make walls ("God opened the sea!"), and all the kids run through to dry land before "Pharaoh" can tag them. When kids are across, the helpers drop the walls. Repeat back the other way.
- **How to win:** Kids who make it across safely shout "The LORD will fight for you!" (Exodus 14:14). Tagged kids become Pharaoh's helpers until the next round.
- **Adaptation:** *Little Lambs:* no tagging, just walk through as the "walls" rise, and sing. *Older kids:* they must name a Law book in order before the walls open.
- **Learning goal:** The Red Sea rescue; trusting God.

### 2. Plague Pop-Up
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers (Little Lambs with a leader).
- **Supplies:** 10 picture cards (one per plague).
- **Setup:** Teach a motion for each plague: (1) blood: wiggle red fingers like a river; (2) frogs: hop; (3) gnats: scratch; (4) flies: buzz and swat; (5) livestock: moo and fall over; (6) boils: "ouch!" on your arms; (7) hail: hands bounce down from the sky; (8) locusts: munch munch; (9) darkness: cover your eyes; (10) firstborn: everyone kneels quietly, and then a leader holds up a red "lamb's blood" door sign and all stand: "Passed over!"
- **How to play:** Kids sit on the floor. The leader flips a card or calls a number. Kids "pop up" and do the motion. Speed up as you go. For a challenge round, call numbers out of order.
- **How to win:** Last one to do the correct motion sits out one round as the "plague caller."
- **Adaptation:** *Older kids:* say each plague in order without cards. *Ambassadors:* match each plague to the Egyptian god it challenged (for example the Nile god Hapi, the frog goddess Heqet, the sun god Ra).
- **Learning goal:** The 10 plagues in order; God's power over false gods.

### 3. Manna Gather
- **Best for:** Little Lambs, Explorers.
- **Supplies:** Lots of cotton balls or white pom-poms (or packing peanuts) scattered across the floor; a small cup per child.
- **Setup:** Scatter the "manna" before kids enter the room (or while they cover their eyes: "It's nighttime!").
- **How to play:** "Morning!" Kids gather manna, but each child can gather only enough to fill their cup once (Exodus 16:16–18). Next round: "It's day six! Gather two cups!" Then: "Sabbath! Rest!" Everyone lies down; no gathering allowed.
- **How to win:** Everyone who obeyed the rules gets a real snack (such as honey graham crackers or vanilla wafers, "like wafers made with honey"). *Check allergies first!*
- **Adaptation:** *Older kids:* hide a few "rotten" (brown) pom-poms; anyone who grabbed more than a cup must take those too (16:20).
- **Learning goal:** God provides daily; trust and obedience.

### 4. Ten Commandment Tower
- **Best for:** All ages.
- **Supplies:** 10 plastic or paper cups per team, numbered 1–10 (or labeled with the commandments for older kids), a ball for knocking towers down at the end.
- **Setup:** Teams line up at a table.
- **How to play:** Each child in turn says one commandment (or one Law book, for younger kids) and adds a cup to the tower (base of 4, then 3, 2, 1). When the tower is complete, the team says all ten together.
- **How to win:** First team with a complete pyramid and all ten said correctly. Bonus fun: the leader rolls a ball to knock the tower down: "The golden calf broke God's law, but God forgave and gave the commands again!" Rebuild.
- **Adaptation:** *Little Lambs:* stack cups while saying "Love God! Love people!" *Ambassadors:* sort into "love God" (1–4) and "love neighbor" (5–10) towers.
- **Learning goal:** Learning the Ten Commandments; God's law is good.

### 5. Spy Hunt for Grapes
- **Best for:** All ages.
- **Supplies:** 30–40 purple paper "grapes" hidden around the room; about 10 green paper "giant" cards hidden too; two paper towel tubes taped into a "pole."
- **Setup:** Split the class into "tribes" of up to 12 spies each.
- **How to play:** Spies search for grapes. Each grape found = 1 point. If you find a "giant" card, you must shout "God is with us! Don't be afraid!" (Numbers 14:9) to keep the card for 3 bonus points. If you just hide it again, it's no points.
- **How to win:** The tribe with the most points, carried back together on their "pole," wins.
- **Adaptation:** *Little Lambs:* find grapes, no giants. *Older kids:* each grape has a trivia question on the back that must be answered to score.
- **Learning goal:** Joshua and Caleb's faith; courage in God.

### 6. Look and Live Tag
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers.
- **Supplies:** A pool noodle or broom with a pipe-cleaner "bronze snake" on top; 2–3 "snake" taggers (with green armbands).
- **Setup:** Open space. One leader stands still holding the pole as "Moses."
- **How to play:** Snakes tag players. A tagged player freezes and holds their arm like it's "bitten." To unfreeze, they must turn and **look** at the bronze snake pole while saying "Look and live!" (Numbers 21:8). Moses may move the pole around so frozen kids have to find it.
- **How to win:** After 2 minutes, count frozen players. Rotate taggers. End by reading John 3:14–15.
- **Adaptation:** *Ambassadors:* run the game for younger kids and give the gospel connection at the end.
- **Learning goal:** The bronze snake and how it points to Jesus on the cross.

### 7. Law Line-Up Relay
- **Best for:** All ages.
- **Supplies:** For each team, 5 large cards: GENESIS, EXODUS, LEVITICUS, NUMBERS, DEUTERONOMY (shuffled), at the far wall.
- **Setup:** Teams of 5 (or groups that rotate).
- **How to play:** On "Go!" each runner brings back one card. Once all 5 are back, the team lines up **in order** holding the cards and sings Part B of the song.
- **How to win:** First team in correct order wins. Second round: line up *backwards*. Third round: cards show the one-word summaries (Beginnings, Rescue, Holy, Wilderness, Remember) instead of names.
- **Adaptation:** *Little Lambs:* cards have the book picture (sunrise, door, praying hands, numbers, heart); a teacher helps line up. *Ambassadors:* add the 3 Phase 3 books as a sneak peek.
- **Learning goal:** The 5 books of the Law in order.

## Activities & Crafts ✂️

### 1. Baby Moses Basket (Week 1)
- **Supplies:** Small paper bowls or cupcake liners (brown), brown yarn or paper strips, a clothespin or a small paper "baby" wrapped in a fabric scrap, green paper strips (reeds), blue paper plates (river), glue.
- **Steps:** (1) Glue brown strips around the bowl to look woven. (2) Put the baby inside. (3) Glue the basket on the blue plate and add green reeds around it. (4) Write "God kept Moses safe. Exodus 2."
- **Age adaptations:** *Little Lambs:* pre-made baskets, add the baby and reeds. *Older kids:* actually weave paper strips; write a prayer for someone who needs God's protection.
- **What it teaches:** God protects and has a plan even in scary times.

### 2. Passover Door (Week 1)
- **Supplies:** Construction paper (brown for a door), red paper strips or red paint with sponge brushes, a lamb sticker or cotton-ball lamb, markers.
- **Steps:** (1) Fold the brown paper so the "door" opens. (2) Glue red strips along the top and both sides (the doorframe). (3) Inside, write: "When I see the blood, I will pass over you. Exodus 12:13" and on the inside back: "Jesus is our Passover Lamb! 1 Corinthians 5:7." (4) Add the lamb.
- **Age adaptations:** *Little Lambs:* red stickers or paint; teacher writes the words. *Ambassadors:* write a short explanation of how the Passover points to the cross.
- **What it teaches:** The Passover lamb and Jesus, the Lamb of God.

### 3. Stone Tablets (Week 2)
- **Supplies:** Gray cardstock cut into two rounded tablets per child (or air-dry clay), markers, brass fastener or tape to join them.
- **Steps:** (1) Number the tablets I–V and VI–X. (2) Write or draw each commandment in kid words. (3) Join the tablets. (4) On the back write "Love God (1–4) · Love people (5–10). Matthew 22:37–39."
- **Age adaptations:** *Little Lambs:* draw a heart (love God) on one and a smiley face (love people) on the other. *Explorers:* write kid-word commandments. *Older:* write the reference for each (Exodus 20:3–17).
- **What it teaches:** The Ten Commandments and Jesus' summary of them.

### 4. Shoebox Tabernacle (Week 2, Trailblazers / Ambassadors)
- **Supplies:** Shoebox (lid off) or a box base, white fabric or paper for the courtyard curtains, a small box for the tent, gold paper for the ark, a bottle cap for the bronze basin, a small cube for the altar, cotton for the cloud, glue, and Exodus 25–27 for reference.
- **Steps:** (1) Line the box with white "curtains" (courtyard). (2) Place the altar and basin near the entrance. (3) Put the tent at the back with a curtain dividing the Holy Place and the Most Holy Place. (4) Put the gold ark inside the Most Holy Place. (5) Top with a cotton cloud.
- **Age adaptations:** *Explorers:* the leader builds it as a class with kids adding pieces. *Ambassadors:* explain each piece's link to Jesus (altar = sacrifice; basin = cleansing; lampstand = light of the world; bread = bread of life; curtain torn when Jesus died, Matthew 27:51).
- **What it teaches:** God wants to live with His people; the way to God is through Jesus.

### 5. Giant Grape Cluster (Week 3)
- **Supplies:** Purple circles (or purple balloons, or bubble wrap painted purple), a green leaf and a paper "pole" (a straw or craft stick), glue.
- **Steps:** (1) Glue purple circles in a big triangle cluster. (2) Glue the stem and leaf at the top. (3) Tape the cluster hanging from a stick. (4) Write "The LORD is with us. Do not be afraid. Numbers 14:9."
- **Age adaptations:** *Little Lambs:* dot purple paint with a cork or finger. *Older:* write a "giant" (a fear) on one grape and a promise of God on another.
- **What it teaches:** Courage that comes from trusting God.

### 6. Bronze Snake on a Pole (Week 3)
- **Supplies:** Brown or gold pipe cleaners, a craft stick or straw, googly eyes (optional), a small card.
- **Steps:** (1) Wrap the pipe cleaner around the stick in a coil. (2) Bend the top into a snake head and add eyes. (3) Attach a card: "Look and live! Numbers 21:8 · Look to Jesus! John 3:14–15."
- **Age adaptations:** *Little Lambs:* pre-made coil; they add the eyes and color the card. *Ambassadors:* explain the connection to a younger buddy.
- **What it teaches:** Salvation comes by looking to Jesus in faith.

### 7. Heart-Door Scroll (Week 4)
- **Supplies:** Paper strips (about 3 x 8 inches), two drinking straws or chopsticks, ribbon, markers; a small decorated paper "door" frame.
- **Steps:** (1) Write Deuteronomy 6:5 on the paper strip (Little Lambs: "Love the LORD your God" traced). (2) Tape a straw to each end and roll it into a scroll. (3) Tie with ribbon. (4) Decorate the paper door and tape the scroll to its frame, "Write them on the doorframes" (6:9). Take it home to hang by the bedroom door.
- **Age adaptations:** *Older kids:* write the whole Shema (6:4–5) in their best handwriting; add a list of "Times our family will talk about God" (meals, car rides, bedtime).
- **What it teaches:** Loving God with all your heart and remembering His Word every day.

### 🧠 Big Questions for Ambassadors (apologetics / worldview)
1. **"Did the exodus really happen? There's no evidence."**
   *Suggested answer:* Ancient Egypt rarely recorded its defeats, and nomads living in tents in a desert leave very little archaeological trace. Still, there's evidence of Semitic peoples living in the Nile delta, Egyptian records of slaves making bricks, and the Merneptah Stele (around 1208 BC), which mentions "Israel" already in Canaan. Most importantly, Israel's whole identity, its festivals (Passover) and its laws all rest on this event; nations don't usually invent a humiliating slave origin. And Jesus treated it as history (John 6:49).
2. **"Aren't the Old Testament laws harsh and outdated?"**
   *Suggested answer:* Many laws were for ancient Israel as a nation and for its temple worship, and Jesus fulfilled those (Matthew 5:17; Hebrews 8:13). Compared with other ancient law codes, Israel's laws were often more humane: protecting foreigners, the poor, widows, orphans and even animals (Leviticus 19:9–10, 33–34; Deuteronomy 24:17–22; 25:4). The heart of the law, loving God and loving neighbor, is never outdated.
3. **"Why would God need blood sacrifices? That seems primitive."**
   *Suggested answer:* Sacrifice shows how serious sin is: it brings death (Romans 6:23). God isn't bloodthirsty; He is just and loving. He provided a substitute so sinners could live (Leviticus 17:11). Animal sacrifices could never fully remove sin (Hebrews 10:4); they pointed ahead to the day God Himself, in Jesus, would give His own life for us. The cross shows both how serious sin is and how great God's love is.
