## The Big Idea (in kid words)

Jesus came back to life, and then He went back up to heaven. But He did not leave His friends alone. He sent the Holy Spirit — God Himself — to live inside everyone who trusts Jesus. The Holy Spirit made scared people brave and gave them power to tell everyone the good news. That group of Jesus-followers is called **the church** — and if you love Jesus, you are part of it too!

**How this points to Jesus:** The book of Acts is really "Part 2" of what Jesus did. Luke wrote that his first book (the Gospel of Luke) told what Jesus *began* to do and teach (Acts 1:1). Acts shows Jesus *still* working — from heaven, through His Spirit, through His people. Every healing in Acts happens "in the name of Jesus," every sermon is about Jesus dying and rising, and the whole book ends with Paul "proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ" (Acts 28:31).

## Teacher Preparation

### Background for the teacher
Acts is the bridge between the Gospels and the Letters. Without it, we would not know how Paul became an apostle, who the churches in Corinth, Philippi, and Ephesus were, or how a small Jewish movement in Jerusalem became a worldwide family including Gentiles (non-Jews). Luke was a careful historian (Luke 1:1–4), and he was an eyewitness for parts of the story — notice the "we" sections starting in Acts 16:10, where Luke travels with Paul.

The book has two main human "heroes": **Peter** (chapters 1–12, mostly among Jewish people) and **Paul** (chapters 13–28, mostly among Gentiles). But the true main character is the **Holy Spirit**, who is mentioned over 50 times. The Spirit is not a "force" or an "it"; He is God, the third person of the Trinity, who comforts, guides, and empowers believers.

### Key truths to emphasize
1. **Jesus is alive and reigning.** The ascension is not Jesus "leaving"; it's Jesus taking His throne.
2. **The Holy Spirit lives in every believer** (Acts 2:38–39). Kids who trust Jesus have the Spirit too — this isn't just for grown-ups or "super Christians."
3. **The church is people, not a building.** The first church met in homes (Acts 2:46).
4. **The gospel is for everyone** — every nation, language, and background (Acts 10:34–35).
5. **God's plan can't be stopped.** Jail, threats, storms, and shipwrecks could not stop the message (Acts 28:31 ends with "without hindrance").

### Timeline & map notes
- **AD 30/33** — Jesus' death, resurrection, ascension, and Pentecost (Pentecost = 50 days after Passover; Jesus ascended 40 days after rising, Acts 1:3).
- **~AD 34–36** — Stephen martyred; Saul's conversion.
- **~AD 46–48** — Paul's 1st journey (Cyprus and southern Turkey: Antioch in Pisidia, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe).
- **~AD 49** — Jerusalem Council (Acts 15).
- **~AD 49–52** — 2nd journey (Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea, Athens, Corinth).
- **~AD 53–57** — 3rd journey (long stay in Ephesus).
- **~AD 57–62** — Arrest in Jerusalem, prison in Caesarea, voyage and shipwreck, house arrest in Rome.

**Map tip:** Draw three loops on a big map of the Mediterranean (blue = 1st journey, green = 2nd, orange = 3rd) and a red dotted line for the trip to Rome. Trailblazers love tracing these.

### Handling sensitive content by age
| Topic | Little Lambs (4–6) | Explorers (7–10) | Trailblazers & Ambassadors (11–17) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stephen's death (Acts 7)** | "Some angry people hurt Stephen very badly, and he died. But Jesus was right there, and Stephen went to be with Jesus." No details of stoning. | "They threw stones at him." Focus on Stephen forgiving them like Jesus did. | Discuss persecution then and now; Stephen's prayer mirrors Jesus' on the cross (Luke 23:34). |
| **Ananias & Sapphira (Acts 5)** | Skip. | Optional; emphasize "God cares about honesty." | Discuss God's holiness and why lying to the Holy Spirit was so serious in the newborn church. |
| **Saul hurting Christians (Acts 8–9)** | "Saul was mean to Jesus' friends." | "He put Christians in jail." | Discuss real forgiveness: God can change anyone. |
| **Beatings, jail, shipwreck** | Keep calm and hopeful: "God took care of them." | Fine to tell plainly; highlight courage and joy. | Discuss suffering for faith and why it doesn't mean God has abandoned us. |

### Common kid questions (with simple answers)
- **"Where did Jesus go?"** — Up to heaven, to be with God the Father. He is alive right now, and He is coming back one day (Acts 1:11).
- **"Is the Holy Spirit a ghost?"** — No. The old word "Holy Ghost" just meant "Holy Spirit." He is God, and He is kind, good, and with us.
- **"Can I see the Holy Spirit?"** — Not with our eyes, like we can't see wind. But we can see what He does — like how wind moves the trees, the Spirit changes people's hearts.
- **"Will I get fire on my head?"** — The flames on Pentecost were a special sign for that day. The Spirit doesn't usually come with flames, but He comes to everyone who trusts Jesus.
- **"Why did God let Stephen die?"** — We don't know every reason. But God was with Stephen, Stephen went to be with Jesus, and God used what happened to spread the good news to new places (Acts 8:4).
- **"Can God change a really mean person?"** — Yes! Saul was one of the meanest enemies of Christians, and God changed him into Paul, one of the greatest missionaries ever.
- **"Am I in the church?"** — If you trust Jesus, yes! The church is God's family, and you belong.

## Weekly Lessons

### Week 1 — Wind, Fire & Power! (Acts 1–2)

**Scripture:** Acts 1:1–11; 2:1–41
**Lesson goal:** Kids will know that Jesus went back to heaven and sent the Holy Spirit to give His followers power to tell others about Him.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "The Invisible Helper"**
*Supplies:* a small handheld fan (or a sheet of cardboard), a pinwheel, a few tissues or feathers, an uninflated balloon.
1. Ask, "Can you see wind?" (No.) Turn on the fan and point it at the pinwheel. "Can you see what the wind DOES?" (Yes!)
2. Blow up the balloon a little: "Air inside makes it big and strong." Let it go and watch it zoom.
3. Say: "Today we'll learn about Someone we can't see with our eyes — the Holy Spirit. Like wind, we see what He does. He fills people and gives them power!"

**Story script (read aloud):**
> After Jesus came back to life, He spent forty days with His friends. He ate with them, talked with them, and taught them about God's kingdom. Then one day, on a hill called the Mount of Olives, Jesus gave them a big job.
>
> "Wait in Jerusalem," Jesus said. "Soon the Holy Spirit will come on you, and you will get power. Then you will be My witnesses — in Jerusalem, in Judea and Samaria, and all the way to the ends of the earth!" A witness is someone who tells what they have seen.
>
> And then — whoosh! — Jesus went up, up, up into the sky until a cloud hid Him. His friends stood there staring. Suddenly two men dressed in white stood beside them. "Why are you looking at the sky?" they said. "This same Jesus will come back the same way you saw Him go!"
>
> So the friends went back to Jerusalem and prayed together and waited. Ten days later, it was a big holiday called Pentecost. Jerusalem was crowded with people from many countries. Jesus' friends were all together in one place.
>
> Suddenly, a sound like a mighty, rushing wind filled the whole house! Then something that looked like little flames of fire came and rested on each person. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit — and they began to speak in other languages they had never learned!
>
> People outside heard the noise and came running. "How can this be?" they said. "These men are from Galilee, but we hear them telling about God's wonders in our own languages!"
>
> Then Peter stood up. Remember Peter? Not long before, he had been so scared that he said three times he didn't even know Jesus. But now, filled with the Holy Spirit, Peter was brave! He shouted, "Jesus, the One you put on a cross, God raised back to life! We have all seen Him! God has made Him both Lord and Messiah!"
>
> The people's hearts hurt. "What should we do?" they asked. Peter said, "Turn away from your sins and be baptized in the name of Jesus. God will forgive you, and you will receive the Holy Spirit too!"
>
> That day, about three thousand people believed in Jesus and were baptized. The church was born — and it was just getting started!

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Make sound effects together: rub hands for wind, wiggle fingers for flames. Repeat: "Jesus went UP (point up), the Spirit came DOWN (wiggle fingers down)."
- **Explorers:** Have them count to 40 (days with Jesus) and then 10 more (waiting) = 50 = Pentecost ("Pente" means fifty). Practice spelling A-C-T-S with claps.
- **Trailblazers:** Compare Babel (Genesis 11 — languages confused, people scattered) and Pentecost (languages understood, people united in Jesus). Look up Acts 2:9–11 and find the countries on a map.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss Peter's transformation (Luke 22:54–62 vs. Acts 2:14). What changed him? Read Acts 2:42–47 and compare that church to ours today. Have one Ambassador lead the Little Lambs' sound-effect retelling.

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) Where did Jesus go? (Up to heaven.) 2) Who did Jesus send to help His friends? (The Holy Spirit.) 3) What did it sound like? (Wind!)
- *Explorers:* 1) What job did Jesus give His friends? 2) What did the people from other countries hear? 3) How was Peter different from the night Jesus was arrested?
- *Trailblazers:* 1) Why do you think God let people hear the message in their own languages? 2) Acts 1:8 lists four places — what does that tell us about God's plan? 3) What does a "witness" do, and how can you be one at school?
- *Ambassadors:* 1) Peter said, "God has made this Jesus… both Lord and Messiah" (2:36). Why are those two titles so important? 2) What do Acts 2:42–47's four habits (teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, prayer) look like in a teenager's life? 3) Why does it matter that the Holy Spirit is a Person and not just a power?

### Week 2 — Brave in Jesus' Name (Acts 3–4; 6–7)

**Scripture:** Acts 3:1–10; 4:1–31; 6:8–15; 7:54–60
**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that the Holy Spirit gives courage to speak about Jesus, even when it's hard.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "What Do I Have?"**
*Supplies:* an empty wallet or purse, a few play coins, a small wrapped "gift" box with a paper heart inside labeled "JESUS."
1. Open the wallet and dump out the coins. "Is money a good gift? Sure! But today we'll meet two men who had *no* money."
2. Hold up the box: "They had something better to give." Open it and show the heart.
3. Say: "Peter said, 'Silver or gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you.' Let's find out what he gave!"

**Story script:**
> One afternoon, Peter and John walked to the temple to pray. By the gate called Beautiful sat a man who had never been able to walk — not even once since the day he was born. He was over forty years old! Every day, friends carried him to the gate so he could beg for money.
>
> "Please, give me some money!" the man called. Peter and John stopped and looked right at him. "Look at us!" Peter said. The man looked up, hoping for coins.
>
> But Peter said, "Silver or gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk!" Peter took his hand and helped him up — and instantly the man's feet and ankles became strong! He jumped up, stood, and walked. Then he went into the temple walking and jumping and praising God!
>
> Everyone stared. "Isn't that the man who used to sit by the gate?" Peter told them, "Why are you surprised? We didn't do this by our own power. Jesus did it! The same Jesus you killed — God raised Him from the dead!"
>
> The leaders were angry. They put Peter and John in jail overnight. The next day they said, "Stop talking about Jesus!" But Peter and John answered, "We cannot help speaking about what we have seen and heard." Then they went back to their friends, and everyone prayed, "Lord, help us speak Your word with great boldness." The place where they were shook, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit again!
>
> More and more people believed. One of the church's helpers was a man named Stephen. He was full of God's grace and power. Some people hated what Stephen said about Jesus, so they dragged him in front of the leaders. Stephen bravely told them the whole story of God's people — and how they had rejected Jesus.
>
> The leaders grew furious. But Stephen looked up to heaven and said, "Look! I see heaven open and Jesus standing at the right hand of God!" They dragged him outside the city and threw stones at him. As he was dying, Stephen prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit… Lord, do not hold this sin against them." Just like Jesus, Stephen forgave the people who hurt him. And then he went to be with Jesus forever.

*(Little Lambs version of the ending: "Some angry people hurt Stephen very badly. But Stephen saw Jesus in heaven, and he forgave them, and he went to be with Jesus.")*

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Act out the lame man: sit on the floor, then on "In the name of Jesus — walk!" everyone jumps up and hops around praising God. Repeat three times; they will beg for more.
- **Explorers:** Hold a "Brave Meter" (thermometer drawn on poster). Ask: "When is it hard to talk about Jesus?" Color up the meter as they share ideas for being brave.
- **Trailblazers:** Compare Stephen's last words (7:59–60) to Jesus' on the cross (Luke 23:34, 46). Why would Luke include these similarities?
- **Ambassadors:** Read Acts 4:13 — the leaders saw that Peter and John were "unschooled, ordinary men" and "took note that these men had been with Jesus." Discuss: courage comes from time with Jesus, not from being impressive. Note that Saul (later Paul) watched Stephen die (7:58; 8:1) — foreshadowing next week.

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) What did the man do after Jesus healed him? (Walked and jumped!) 2) Whose name did Peter use? (Jesus.) 3) Who did Stephen see in heaven? (Jesus.)
- *Explorers:* 1) Why didn't Peter give the man money? 2) What did Peter and John say when they were told to stop talking about Jesus? 3) How was Stephen like Jesus?
- *Trailblazers:* 1) Why did the healing make the leaders angry instead of happy? 2) What did the believers pray for in Acts 4:29 — and what didn't they pray for? 3) What's one hard place where you need courage to follow Jesus?
- *Ambassadors:* 1) The believers prayed for boldness, not safety. What does that teach us about prayer? 2) How can you forgive someone who mistreats you for your faith? 3) Stephen's death led to the gospel spreading (8:1–4). How has God brought good out of hard things in history or in your life?

### Week 3 — From Enemy to Friend (Acts 8:26–40; 9:1–31; 10)

**Scripture:** Acts 8:26–40; 9:1–31; 10:1–48
**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that God can change anyone's heart and that the good news is for every person, everywhere.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "Dirty to Clean"**
*Supplies:* a clear glass of water, a few drops of food coloring (dark blue), a small bottle of bleach-free stain remover or a few drops of household iodine + vitamin C tablet (adult-handled; the classic "disappearing color" trick), OR simply a dirty T-shirt and a clean T-shirt.
- Simple version: Show a muddy shirt. "Could this ever be clean again?" Then hold up the same style shirt, clean. "God can make a brand-new person out of someone who was really, really wrong."
- Say: "Today you'll meet a man named Saul who hated Jesus' friends. Could God ever change *him*?"

**Story script:**
> After Stephen died, a young man named Saul got angrier and angrier at the people who followed Jesus. He went from house to house, dragging Christians to jail. Then he asked for letters so he could arrest Jesus-followers in the city of Damascus too.
>
> Saul and his men were on the road, almost to Damascus, when suddenly — FLASH! — a light from heaven, brighter than the noon sun, blazed all around him. Saul fell to the ground. A voice said, "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute Me?"
>
> "Who are You, Lord?" Saul asked, trembling.
>
> "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting," the voice answered. "Now get up and go into the city, and you will be told what to do."
>
> Saul stood up and opened his eyes — but he couldn't see anything! He was blind. His men had to lead him by the hand into Damascus. For three days Saul couldn't see, and he didn't eat or drink. He just prayed.
>
> In Damascus there was a believer named Ananias. Jesus told him in a vision, "Go to Saul and pray for him." Ananias gulped. "Lord, this man has done terrible things to Your people!" But Jesus said, "Go! I have chosen him to tell the nations about Me."
>
> So Ananias went. He put his hands on Saul and said, "Brother Saul" — brother! — "Jesus sent me so you can see again and be filled with the Holy Spirit." Right away, something like scales fell from Saul's eyes. He could see! He got up and was baptized.
>
> Saul began telling everyone, "Jesus is the Son of God!" Now people wanted to hurt *him*. One night his friends put him in a big basket and lowered him through an opening in the city wall so he could escape. Later, Saul became known by his other name — Paul — and he became one of the greatest missionaries who ever lived.
>
> Meanwhile, God was teaching Peter something new too. A Roman soldier named Cornelius loved God, and an angel told him to send for Peter. At the same time, Peter had a vision of a big sheet full of animals, and God said, "Do not call anything impure that God has made clean." When Peter met Cornelius and his family, he understood: "God does not show favoritism!" The good news of Jesus is for everybody — every nation, every color, every language. And the Holy Spirit came on Cornelius's whole household too!

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Turn the lights off for "blind" and on when the "scales fall off." Say together: "Jesus can change ANYONE!" Hold up a heart that flips from a frowny face to a smiley face.
- **Explorers:** Point out Saul = Paul: same man, two names (Saul was his Hebrew name; Paul his Roman name — Acts 13:9). Let them spell both.
- **Trailblazers:** Look up Acts 9:15 and Galatians 1:13–16 — Paul's own description of his change. Map Jerusalem → Damascus (about 135 miles / 220 km).
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss why Cornelius's story was such a big deal: the first church was mostly Jewish, and including Gentiles without them becoming Jewish first was a major step (Acts 10–11; 15). Discuss Ananias's courage — would you visit someone who was known to hurt Christians?

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) Who talked to Saul from heaven? (Jesus.) 2) What happened to Saul's eyes? (He couldn't see, then he could!) 3) Can Jesus change anybody? (Yes!)
- *Explorers:* 1) Why was Ananias afraid to visit Saul? 2) How did Saul escape from Damascus? 3) What did Peter learn from Cornelius?
- *Trailblazers:* 1) Jesus said Saul was persecuting *Me*. What does that tell us about how Jesus feels about His people? 2) Why do you think Ananias called him "Brother Saul"? 3) Who is someone you think could "never" change? Pray for them this week.
- *Ambassadors:* 1) Paul later called himself "the worst of sinners" (1 Timothy 1:15–16). How does his story give hope to others? 2) What does "God does not show favoritism" (10:34) mean for racism or cliques today? 3) What does true conversion look like — a feeling, a decision, a changed life, or all of these?

### Week 4 — To the Ends of the Earth (Acts 13–14; 16; 27–28)

**Scripture:** Acts 13:1–3; 16:6–40; 27:1–28:31
**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that God sent Paul around the world with the good news, and nothing — not jail, storms, or snakes — could stop it.

**Opening hook / object lesson — "Can't Stop It!"**
*Supplies:* a roll of masking tape, a small ball, a paper "message" taped to the ball reading "JESUS IS ALIVE!"
1. Lay tape lines on the floor as "walls." Roll the ball; let kids try to block it with their hands, but have a teacher keep finding new ways around.
2. Say: "Some people tried to stop the message about Jesus. They tried jail. They tried chains. Even a giant storm tried! But God's message kept rolling."

**Story script:**
> In the city of Antioch, the church was praying when the Holy Spirit said, "Set apart Barnabas and Saul for the work I have called them to." So the church prayed for them and sent them off. That was the first missionary journey! A missionary is someone sent to tell others about Jesus.
>
> Paul went on three big journeys, walking and sailing thousands of miles. On his second trip, he took his friend Silas. They went to a city called Philippi. There a woman named Lydia, who sold fancy purple cloth, believed in Jesus. But some men got angry at Paul and Silas, and they had them beaten and thrown into the deepest part of the jail with their feet locked in wooden blocks.
>
> Do you know what Paul and Silas did at midnight? They prayed and sang songs to God! The other prisoners were listening. Suddenly — RUMBLE! — a huge earthquake shook the jail. All the doors flew open, and everyone's chains came loose!
>
> The jailer woke up and thought all the prisoners had escaped. He was terrified. But Paul shouted, "Don't harm yourself! We are all here!" The jailer rushed in, shaking, and fell down before them. "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?"
>
> They answered, "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved — you and your household." That very night, the jailer and his whole family believed and were baptized, and he was filled with joy!
>
> Years later, Paul was arrested in Jerusalem. He asked to have his case heard by Caesar, the emperor in Rome. So soldiers put Paul on a ship. But a terrible storm blew for two weeks. The sailors threw cargo overboard. Everyone thought they would die. Then Paul stood up and said, "Keep up your courage! An angel of God told me that not one of you will be lost — only the ship."
>
> The ship crashed on the island of Malta and broke apart. But all 276 people made it safely to shore! While Paul was building a fire, a poisonous snake bit his hand. Everyone waited for him to fall over — but Paul just shook it off into the fire and was fine!
>
> Finally Paul reached Rome. He was under guard in a house for two years, but people came to visit him every day. And the book of Acts ends with Paul telling everyone about Jesus "with all boldness and without hindrance." The good news was unstoppable — and it still is! Now it has reached all the way to us.

**Age-group teaching tips**
- **Little Lambs:** Play "jail": sit with "chained" hands (crossed wrists). Sing "Jesus Loves Me" softly, then stomp feet for the earthquake and throw hands apart — "Free!" Then rock side to side for the storm.
- **Explorers:** Let them shout the sound effects: RUMBLE! CRASH! HISS (snake)! Give each child a "ticket" for each city visited and stamp it.
- **Trailblazers:** Trace the three journeys on the map. Find Philippi, Ephesus, Corinth, and Rome — and connect: "Paul later wrote letters to these very churches!" (Next month's books.)
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss Acts 17:16–34 (Paul in Athens with philosophers). How did Paul start with what they knew ("an unknown god") to point them to Jesus? Practice explaining the gospel in 60 seconds to a friend who doesn't go to church.

**Discussion questions**
- *Little Lambs:* 1) What did Paul and Silas do in jail? (Sang and prayed!) 2) What shook the jail? (An earthquake.) 3) What bit Paul? (A snake — and he was okay!)
- *Explorers:* 1) What question did the jailer ask? 2) How many people were on Paul's ship, and how many were saved? 3) Why do you think Paul and Silas could sing in a dark jail?
- *Trailblazers:* 1) Why do you think God let Paul go through so many hard things? 2) How did Paul encourage others during the storm? 3) Acts ends without telling us what happened to Paul at his trial. Why might Luke end it there?
- *Ambassadors:* 1) Acts ends "without hindrance" while Paul is literally in chains. What's the point? 2) Where are "the ends of the earth" for you — who near you hasn't heard about Jesus? 3) How do you answer someone who says, "If God loved Paul, why did he suffer so much?"

### The syllable-clap method
1. Say the word slowly.
2. Clap once for each syllable: *Acts* (1 clap) · *A-pos-tle* (3 claps) · *Pen-te-cost* (3 claps) · *Mis-sion-ar-y* (4 claps) · *Per-se-cu-tion* (4 claps).
3. Spell each syllable, then put it together.

### "Spell It Out" relay
*Supplies:* Large letter cards (one letter per card) for several words, two baskets, masking tape start line.
1. Divide into two teams. Put the scrambled letters for one word in each team's basket across the room.
2. Teacher calls a word (e.g., "APOSTLE"). One player at a time runs, grabs one correct letter, and runs back.
3. The team lines up their letters in order on the floor. First team to spell it correctly and read it aloud wins a point.
4. Little Lambs pair with an older buddy; Ambassadors spell harder words ("PERSECUTION").

## Games

### 🎮 1. Wind Relay
- **Best for:** All ages (great for Little Lambs & Explorers)
- **Supplies:** Cotton balls or feather pieces, straws (one per child), masking tape, two paper "flame" targets
- **Setup:** Tape a start line and a finish line about 10–15 feet apart. Put a paper flame at the finish for each team.
- **How to play:** Teams line up. Each player blows a cotton ball through a straw from start to finish (no hands!), then runs back and tags the next player. While waiting, the team chants the New Testament books.
- **How to win:** First team to have every player finish wins. Bonus point if the team can say Acts 1:8.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* blow with mouths (no straws) and shorten the distance. *Older:* blow the cotton ball around obstacles (cones = "the ends of the earth").
- **Learning goal:** We can't see wind, but we see what it does — like the Holy Spirit.

### 🎮 2. Silver & Gold Freeze
- **Best for:** Little Lambs & Explorers
- **Supplies:** Music player, worship song
- **Setup:** Kids sit on the floor as the "man who couldn't walk."
- **How to play:** Teacher (as Peter) says, "Silver or gold I do not have…" Kids stay seated. When the teacher says "…In the name of Jesus — WALK!" everyone jumps up and walks/leaps/dances while music plays. When the music stops, freeze! Teacher calls out a book name; frozen kids must shout the book that comes after it (or before it for older kids).
- **How to win:** Everyone who answers stays in; last few standing are "Brave Witnesses."
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* just freeze and say "Thank You, Jesus!" *Older:* name the section of the called book too.
- **Learning goal:** Healing came through Jesus' name; book-order recall.

### 🎮 3. Road to Damascus Blindfold Walk
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers, Ambassadors
- **Supplies:** Blindfolds (or kids close eyes), cones/chairs for an obstacle path, a "Damascus" sign
- **Setup:** Make a winding path from "Jerusalem" to "Damascus."
- **How to play:** Pairs: one is "Saul" (blindfolded), one is a guide who leads only with words (or by holding a hand for younger kids). At each cone, Saul must say one book of the Bible in order (starting from Genesis) before taking the next step. At Damascus, the guide (as "Ananias") says "Brother Saul, receive your sight!" and removes the blindfold. Switch roles.
- **How to win:** Time each pair; fastest correct run wins — but no skipping books!
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* hold hands; just say "Jesus loves me" at each cone. *Older:* say the books backward from Acts.
- **Learning goal:** Saul's blindness and healing; trusting a guide; recall of book order.

### 🎮 4. Shipwreck!
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Supplies:** Open space, four signs on walls: "BOW" (front), "STERN" (back), "PORT" (left), "STARBOARD" (right)
- **How to play:** Teacher is "Captain." Commands: "Bow!" "Stern!" "Port!" "Starboard!" (run to that wall). "STORM!" (everyone spins in place). "Throw the cargo!" (pretend to toss boxes). "Snake!" (shake your hand off like Paul). "SHIPWRECK!" — everyone must find a group of exactly the number the captain calls ("Shipwreck — groups of 4!") and sit down in a "lifeboat." Each lifeboat must then shout one book from the New Testament.
- **How to win:** Kids left out of a group or too slow help the Captain give the next command (no one is ever really "out" — Acts 27: everyone was saved!).
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* fewer commands; just Storm, Snake, and Shipwreck. *Older:* lifeboats must recite the whole Gospels + Acts or a section of the Old Testament.
- **Learning goal:** Paul's shipwreck story; God kept all 276 people safe.

### 🎮 5. Spread the Good News (Tag)
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers
- **Supplies:** Red pinnies or red bandanas (flames), open area
- **How to play:** Start with one "Witness" holding a red bandana (the Spirit's flame). When the Witness tags someone, they must say a book of the Bible (in order — the class keeps track), and then that person gets a bandana and becomes a Witness too. Keep going until everyone is a Witness. Like the church in Acts, the good news spreads person to person!
- **How to win:** Last person tagged gets to start the next round. Challenge: get through all 44 books before everyone is tagged.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* walk instead of run; say "Jesus loves you!" when tagging. *Older:* add "zones" (Jerusalem, Judea/Samaria, the ends of the earth) that must be reached in order.
- **Learning goal:** Acts 1:8 — the message spreads outward; book-order recall.

### 🎮 6. Acts Trivia Ship Race (Review Sunday)
- **Best for:** All ages, in mixed teams
- **Supplies:** Map of the Mediterranean drawn on poster board with a path of 10 spaces from Jerusalem to Rome, paper boat markers for each team, trivia questions from above
- **How to play:** Teams take turns answering trivia. Easy = move 1 space, Medium = 2, Hard = 3. "Storm" cards (wrong answers) don't send you back — instead the team must say Acts 1:8 together to "stay afloat."
- **How to win:** First team to reach Rome!
- **Learning goal:** Review the whole book of Acts.

## Activities & Crafts

### ✂️ 1. Pentecost Flame Headbands (Week 1)
- **Supplies:** Sentence strips or paper strips (for headbands), red/orange/yellow tissue paper or construction paper, glue sticks, stapler, markers

### ✂️ 2. "In Jesus' Name" Walking Feet Craft (Week 2)
- **Supplies:** Construction paper, crayons, scissors, glue, a long paper strip
- **Steps:** 1) Trace each child's feet on paper and cut out. 2) On one foot write "Walking," on the other "Jumping." 3) Glue to a strip that says "and praising God!" (Acts 3:8).
- **Ages:** *Little Lambs:* teachers trace and cut; kids color. *Explorers:* add Acts 3:6. *Older:* write one place their feet will go this week where they can be brave for Jesus.
- **What it teaches:** Jesus' power healed the man; we can walk in Jesus' name.

### ✂️ 3. Scales-Falling Paper Glasses (Week 3)
- **Supplies:** Paper glasses template (or pipe cleaners shaped as glasses), wax paper or cellophane, tape, sequins or paper "scales"
- **Steps:** 1) Make simple glasses frames. 2) Lightly tape paper "scales" over the lenses. 3) Retell the story; on "Brother Saul, receive your sight!" kids peel the scales off and look through clear lenses.
- **Ages:** *Little Lambs:* pre-made frames. *Older:* write "Before" and "After" words for Saul on each arm of the glasses.
- **What it teaches:** Jesus changed Saul; God opens our eyes to the truth.

### ✂️ 4. Missionary Journey Map & Passport (Week 4)
- **Supplies:** Printed outline map of the Mediterranean (or hand-drawn), colored yarn or markers, small booklet (folded paper), stamps or stickers
- **Steps:** 1) Mark Jerusalem, Antioch, Philippi, Athens, Corinth, Ephesus, Malta, Rome. 2) Glue yarn along Paul's route. 3) In a mini "passport," kids draw something that happened in each city (Philippi = jail, Malta = snake, Rome = house arrest). Stamp each page.
- **Ages:** *Little Lambs:* place stickers on a pre-drawn route. *Explorers:* label cities. *Trailblazers:* color-code the three journeys. *Ambassadors:* add the letters Paul later wrote to each city (Philippians, Corinthians, Ephesians, Thessalonians).
- **What it teaches:** The gospel spread "to the ends of the earth"; Acts connects to Paul's letters.

### ✂️ 5. "Unstoppable" Prayer Chain
- **Supplies:** Strips of paper, markers, tape or glue
- **Steps:** Each child writes or draws the name of someone they want to tell about Jesus, then links their strip to the class chain. Add to it every week. On Review Sunday, hang it around the room and pray over it.
- **Ages:** *Little Lambs:* draw a face. *Older:* write a name and one way to show them Jesus' love.
- **What it teaches:** We are witnesses; the church grows one person at a time.

### ✂️ 6. Shipwreck Snack (bonus)

- **What it teaches:** God kept everyone safe; the snake couldn't hurt Paul.

### 🧠 Big Questions for Ambassadors (apologetics & worldview)
1. **"How do we know the resurrection really happened — wasn't it just made up later?"**
   *Suggested answer:* Acts shows the apostles preaching the resurrection in Jerusalem within weeks — the very city where Jesus' tomb was. If the body were still there, opponents could have produced it. The disciples went from hiding to boldly risking prison and death; people don't usually die for something they know is a lie. Paul, a former enemy, became a believer after meeting the risen Jesus. Early sources (like 1 Corinthians 15:3–8) list many eyewitnesses.
2. **"Is Acts real history, or just a religious story?"**
   *Suggested answer:* Luke names real rulers (Herod Agrippa, Gallio, Felix, Festus, King Agrippa II), cities, and local titles that historians and archaeologists have confirmed (for example, the Gallio inscription at Delphi helps date Paul's time in Corinth). Luke says he carefully investigated (Luke 1:1–4) and was present for parts of the journey ("we" passages). That doesn't prove every miracle by itself, but it shows Luke was a careful, trustworthy historian.
3. **"Isn't it arrogant to say everyone needs Jesus?"**
   *Suggested answer:* Peter said salvation is found in no one else (Acts 4:12), but the message was offered humbly and freely to everyone — Jews, Samaritans, Ethiopians, Romans, Greeks. The gospel isn't "we're better than you"; it's "we were all lost, and Jesus rescues anyone who trusts Him." Sharing good news you believe is true is an act of love, not arrogance.
