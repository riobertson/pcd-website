## The Big Idea (in kid words)

The Minor Prophets are **twelve** messengers with **short** books. "Minor" means *small book*, not *small message*! Some of them warned, "Turn back to God!" Some said, "God loves you even when you run away," just like Jonah found out inside a big fish. Then, after Malachi, God was quiet for about **400 years**. But God wasn't gone. He was getting the world ready for **Jesus**!

**How this points to Jesus:** These little books are packed with big clues about Jesus. **Micah** said the King would be born in **Bethlehem**. **Zechariah** said the King would ride into Jerusalem on a **donkey**. **Jonah** spent three days in the fish, and Jesus said that was a picture of His three days in the tomb. **Malachi**, the very last book of the Old Testament, said, "I will send my messenger, who will prepare the way." That messenger was John the Baptist, who got people ready for Jesus. The Old Testament ends by pointing straight ahead to Jesus!

## The Books in Easy Words

> **Teacher tip:** In the Hebrew Bible, these twelve were copied together on **one scroll** called "The Book of the Twelve." A good way to remember them is in **three groups of four**:
> - **Group 1:** Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah ("**H-J-A-O**": "**H**appy **J**ellyfish **A**te **O**ranges")
> - **Group 2:** Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk ("**J-M-N-H**": "**J**onah **M**et **N**ine **H**ungry fish")
> - **Group 3:** Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi ("**Z-H-Z-M**": "**Z**ebras **H**op, **Z**ebras **M**arch")

## Teacher Preparation

### Background for the teacher

**Why "Minor"?** "Minor" means *shorter*, not *less important*. Their combined length is about the size of Isaiah. In the Hebrew Bible they were copied onto a single scroll called "The Twelve."

**Order isn't strictly chronological.** The twelve are arranged roughly by time, with the earlier (Assyrian-era) prophets first and the post-exile prophets last, but not perfectly. Here's a simple timeline to keep in your head:

| Era | Approx. date (B.C.) | Prophets | Main audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assyrian era** (before the North fell in 722) | 780–700 | Jonah, Amos, Hosea, Micah (Joel's date uncertain) | Jonah → Nineveh; Amos & Hosea → Israel (North); Micah → Judah (South) |
| **Before Babylon** (Judah alone) | 663–600 | Nahum, Zephaniah, Habakkuk | Nahum → Nineveh's fall; Zephaniah & Habakkuk → Judah |
| **After Jerusalem falls** | after 586 | Obadiah | Edom |
| **After the return home** | 520–430 | Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi | The returned exiles in Jerusalem |

**Connections to earlier phases:**
- Jonah and Amos ministered under **Jeroboam II** (2 Kings 14:23–25), from Phase 4.
- Micah ministered under **Hezekiah**, alongside **Isaiah** (Phase 7). Jeremiah 26:18 quotes Micah!
- Zephaniah ministered under **Josiah**, the boy king who found the Book of the Law (Phase 4).
- Haggai and Zechariah are named in **Ezra 5:1 and 6:14** (Phase 5); they encouraged the rebuilding of the temple, finished in 516 B.C.
- Malachi likely ministered around the time of **Nehemiah** (Phase 5); both deal with careless worship, tithing and marriage problems.

**Recurring themes in the Twelve:**
1. **The Day of the LORD:** a coming day when God sets everything right, bad news for the proud and wicked, good news for the humble (Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Zephaniah, Malachi).
2. **God's covenant love (hesed):** God's loyal, never-quitting love (Hosea, Micah 7:18–20, Jonah 4:2).
3. **Justice for the poor:** God hates religious show without kindness and fairness (Amos, Micah).
4. **God over all nations:** God cares about Nineveh, judges Edom and Assyria, and plans to bless all nations (Jonah, Nahum, Obadiah, Zechariah 8:20–23).
5. **The coming King and His messenger** (Micah 5, Zechariah 9–14, Malachi 3–4).

### 🌉 Bridge background: the 400 "Silent" Years (teacher notes)

"Silent" means **no new inspired prophet or Scripture** between Malachi and John the Baptist. It does **not** mean God was absent. Daniel had already predicted some of these empires (Daniel 2, 7, 8, 11). Here is a simple timeline:

| Approx. date | What happened | Why it matters for the Gospels |
|---|---|---|
| ~430 B.C. | Malachi's ministry; Nehemiah's governorship; Persia rules | The Old Testament closes. |
| 333–332 B.C. | **Alexander the Great** (Greece) conquers the Persian Empire, including Judea | **Greek** becomes the common language of the Mediterranean world; the New Testament will be written in everyday (Koine) Greek. |
| 323 B.C. | Alexander dies; his generals split his empire. The **Ptolemies** (Egypt) control Judea for about a century. | |
| ~250 B.C. (3rd century) | Jewish scholars in Alexandria, Egypt, begin translating the Hebrew Scriptures into Greek: the **Septuagint** (often abbreviated LXX). | Non-Jews could now read God's Word; the apostles often quoted it. |
| 198 B.C. | The **Seleucids** (Syria) take control of Judea. | |
| 167 B.C. | Seleucid king **Antiochus IV Epiphanes** outlaws Jewish worship and defiles the temple with a pagan altar and sacrifice. | Compare Daniel 11:31. |
| 167–164 B.C. | The **Maccabean revolt**: a priest named Mattathias and his sons, led by **Judas Maccabeus** ("the Hammer"), fight back. In 164 B.C. they cleanse and rededicate the temple. | This is the origin of **Hanukkah**, the "Festival of Dedication," which Jesus attended (John 10:22–23). |
| 164–63 B.C. | Jewish self-rule under the Hasmonean family. Groups like the **Pharisees** and **Sadducees** form; **synagogues** spread. | These groups and synagogues appear all through the Gospels. |
| 63 B.C. | Roman general **Pompey** captures Jerusalem; **Rome** takes over. | Roman roads, Roman peace (Pax Romana), Roman soldiers, taxes and crosses appear in the Gospels. |
| 37 B.C. | **Herod the Great**, appointed by Rome, becomes king of Judea (ruling until 4 B.C.); about 20/19 B.C. he begins a massive rebuilding of the temple. | The Herod of Matthew 2; the temple of the Gospels (John 2:20, "forty-six years to build"). |
| 27 B.C. | **Caesar Augustus** becomes the first Roman emperor. | His census brings Mary and Joseph to Bethlehem (Luke 2:1). |
| ~6–5 B.C. | Gabriel appears to **Zechariah the priest** in the temple (Luke 1). | **The silence is broken!** |
| ~6–4 B.C. | **Jesus is born** in Bethlehem (Herod died in 4 B.C., so Jesus was born a little before that). | "When the set time had fully come, God sent his Son" (Galatians 4:4). |

**Three ways God "prepared the stage":** (1) **One language** (Greek) for spreading the gospel; (2) **Roads and peace** (Rome) for travel; (3) **A hungry heart**: synagogues full of Scripture and people longing for the Messiah (Luke 2:25, 38).

**A note on the Apocrypha.** Catholic and Orthodox Bibles include some books written during this period (such as 1 and 2 Maccabees), called the Apocrypha or Deuterocanonical books. Protestant Christians value some of them as helpful history (1 Maccabees is a key source for the Hanukkah story), but don't count them as Scripture, in line with the Hebrew Bible Jesus used. If a kid asks, keep it simple and respectful: "Some Bibles include extra history books from this time. In our church we read them as history, not as God's Word."

### Key truths to emphasize
- **God's love doesn't quit** (Hosea, Jonah).
- **God cares how we treat others**: fairness, kindness, humility (Amos, Micah 6:8).
- **We can bring God our hard questions** and still trust Him (Habakkuk).
- **God gives second chances**, to Jonah and to Nineveh.
- **Put God first** (Haggai, Malachi).
- **God keeps His promises on His schedule**: 400 years of waiting ended with Jesus right on time.

### Common kid questions (with simple answers)

1. **"Could a person really live inside a fish?"** God made the fish and sent it to save Jonah (Jonah 1:17). It was a miracle. Jesus talked about Jonah as a real event (Matthew 12:40–41), so we believe it too. The Bible says "a huge fish," not specifically a whale.
2. **"Why did Jonah run away?"** Nineveh was the capital of Assyria, an enemy nation that was very cruel. Jonah didn't want God to forgive them (Jonah 4:2). God wanted to show that His love is for everyone.
3. **"What does 'walk humbly' mean?"** It means living close to God, knowing He is the boss, and not acting like we're better than other people.
4. **"Why was God quiet for 400 years?"** God was waiting for exactly the right time (Galatians 4:4). He had already said everything the people needed until the Messiah came. He was quietly getting the world ready.
5. **"Is God quiet now?"** No! God has spoken to us through His Son, Jesus (Hebrews 1:1–2), and through the Bible, and the Holy Spirit lives in everyone who trusts Jesus.
6. **"What's a tithe?"** A tithe means "a tenth." God's people gave a tenth of their crops and animals to God. Malachi said bringing it showed they trusted God to provide.
7. **"Who is the Elijah Malachi talks about?"** Jesus said it was John the Baptist, who came "in the spirit and power of Elijah" (Luke 1:17; Matthew 11:14). He wasn't Elijah come back to life, but a prophet like him.
8. **"Why do Zechariah and Malachi seem confusing?"** They have visions and symbols, kind of like a picture puzzle. We focus on the clear parts, especially the promises about Jesus.

## Weekly Lessons

### Week 1: "Love That Won't Quit" (Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah)

**Scripture:** Hosea 3:1–5; 11:1–4; Joel 2:12–13, 28; Amos 5:24; 7:7–8; Obadiah 15

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn the first four Minor Prophets, that God's love for His people never quits (Hosea), that God wants our hearts to turn back to Him (Joel), and that God cares about fairness (Amos) and humility (Obadiah).

**Opening hook / object lesson: "The Rubber Band Love"**
- *Supplies:* A sturdy rubber band, a toy heart or red paper heart, a string with a weight tied on (a washer or key) to be a "plumb line," a ruler.
- *Do:* Stretch the rubber band far: "No matter how far it stretches, it pulls back to where it belongs." Say: "God's love is like that. When His people ran far away, His love pulled them back. Today we meet a prophet whose own family showed God's never-quitting love." Then hold up the plumb line: "Builders use this to see if a wall is straight. God used it to show a prophet named Amos something too!"

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> Once there was a prophet named Hosea. God told him to do something very hard. "Hosea, marry a woman named Gomer, and keep loving her, even though she won't always love you back."
>
> Hosea obeyed. He married Gomer, and they had three children. But Gomer stopped loving Hosea. She ran away and chased after other things she thought would make her happy. That broke Hosea's heart.
>
> Then God said something surprising: "Go, show your love to your wife again." Hosea found Gomer. She was in a lot of trouble and had become like a slave. So Hosea paid fifteen pieces of silver and some barley to buy her back! He brought her home and said, "You are mine, and I am yours."
>
> Why did God ask Hosea to do this? Because it was a picture of God's own love! God's people kept running away from Him and loving fake gods. But God said, "When Israel was a child, I loved him… I led them with cords of human kindness, with ties of love." God would not give up on them.
>
> God sent other messengers too. A prophet named **Joel** saw a giant swarm of locusts, grasshopper-like bugs, eat every plant in the land. Joel said, "Return to the LORD your God, for he is gracious and compassionate!" And God made a wonderful promise through Joel: "I will pour out my Spirit on all people."
>
> Next, God chose a shepherd and farmer named **Amos**. Amos was not a fancy preacher. He took care of sheep and fig trees! But God sent him to the rich people of Israel, who were being unfair to the poor. God showed Amos a plumb line, a string with a weight that hangs perfectly straight. God said, "I'm measuring my people. Are they straight or crooked?" Amos shouted, "Let justice roll on like a river!"
>
> Finally, the prophet **Obadiah** wrote the shortest book in the Old Testament, just one chapter! He warned the proud nation of Edom, "You cheered when Jerusalem was hurt. As you have done, it will be done to you."
>
> Hosea, Joel, Amos and Obadiah all said: Come back to God. Be fair. Be humble. And God's love will not quit. Years later, Jesus came to buy us back, not with silver, but with His own life on the cross. That's love that never quits!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Keep it to the main idea: *God loves me even when I run away.* Play "run and come back": kids "run" (walk) to the wall, then you call "Come back, I love you!" and they run back for a hug (high five).
- **Explorers:** Learn Group 1 with the sentence "Happy Jellyfish Ate Oranges" (Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah). Make a real plumb line with string and a washer and check the classroom door.
- **Trailblazers:** Read Hosea 3 and 1 Peter 1:18–19 (redeemed "not with perishable things such as silver or gold… but with the precious blood of Christ"). Read Acts 2:16–21 and see Joel quoted at Pentecost.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss Amos 5:21–24: God rejected worship that came with injustice. What would Amos say to churches today? Discuss Obadiah's warning about gloating over enemies (compare Proverbs 24:17).

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) Did Hosea keep loving his wife? (Yes!) (2) Does God keep loving you? (Yes!) (3) What bugs did Joel see? (Locusts / grasshoppers!)
- *Explorers:* (1) What did Hosea pay to bring Gomer home? (Fifteen silver pieces and barley.) (2) What was Amos's job before he was a prophet? (Shepherd and fig farmer.) (3) Which book is only one chapter long? (Obadiah.)
- *Trailblazers:* (1) How is Hosea's story like the gospel? (2) What does a plumb line show, and what was God "measuring"? (3) Where is Joel 2:28 quoted in the New Testament?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Why do you think God used a marriage to picture His covenant? (2) What injustices would Amos speak against today? (3) How do you respond when someone you don't like gets in trouble?

### Week 2: "Jonah Runs, God Rescues" (Jonah, Micah, Nahum)

**Scripture:** Jonah 1–4 (key verses 1:1–3, 17; 2:9–10; 3:10; 4:2, 11); Micah 5:2; 6:8; Nahum 1:7

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that God gives second chances, that God loves all people (even enemies), that Micah said Jesus would be born in Bethlehem, and that God is a safe place in trouble (Nahum).

**Opening hook / object lesson: "Which Way?"**
- *Supplies:* Two signs: "NINEVEH →" and "← TARSHISH," a toy boat or paper boat, a spray bottle of water (for "storm" mist, optional), a blue sheet.
- *Do:* Put the signs on opposite walls. "God told someone to go THIS way (point to Nineveh). Which way do you think he went?" Have a volunteer walk toward Tarshish with the boat. "Uh oh. Let's find out what happens when we run from God!"

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> God spoke to a prophet named Jonah. "Go to the great city of Nineveh and preach against it, because the people there are doing very wicked things."
>
> But Jonah did NOT want to go to Nineveh. The people there were enemies of Israel, and they were mean and cruel. So Jonah ran away! He went down to the port city of Joppa and got on a ship going to Tarshish, the opposite direction, as far away as he could go!
>
> But you can't run away from God. God sent a great wind, and a huge storm crashed over the sea. *Whoosh! Crash!* The ship was about to break apart! The sailors were terrified. They threw their cargo overboard. They prayed to their gods. But Jonah was asleep down below!
>
> The captain woke him up. "How can you sleep? Get up and call on your God!"
>
> The sailors figured out that the storm was because of Jonah. Jonah said, "I worship the LORD, the God who made the sea and the dry land. I'm running from Him. Throw me into the sea, and it will become calm."
>
> The sailors didn't want to, but finally they did. *SPLASH!* And immediately the sea became calm.
>
> But God wasn't done with Jonah. God sent a huge fish to swallow him up! *GULP!* Jonah was inside the fish for three days and three nights. Inside that dark, smelly fish, Jonah prayed. "In my distress I called to the LORD, and he answered me… Salvation comes from the LORD!"
>
> Then God commanded the fish, and it spit Jonah out onto dry land. *BLEH!*
>
> God said again, "Go to Nineveh." This time Jonah went! He walked through the city shouting, "Forty more days and Nineveh will be overthrown!" And guess what? The people of Nineveh believed God! From the king all the way down to the poorest person, they said sorry to God and stopped doing evil. Even the king got off his throne and sat in the dust! So God had mercy and didn't destroy them.
>
> But Jonah was grumpy! "I knew you were gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and full of love," he complained. Jonah wanted his enemies punished. God gently taught Jonah: "Shouldn't I care about this great city with so many people?"
>
> God loves everyone, even people we don't like. Jesus said that Jonah's three days in the fish were a picture of His own three days in the tomb. Then Jesus came back to life, so that people from every city and nation could be saved!

*Add-on (1 minute):* "Two more prophets this week! **Micah** told us something amazing: the King would be born in little **Bethlehem**, and that's exactly where Jesus was born! And Micah gave us our memory verse: act justly, love mercy, walk humbly with your God. **Nahum** said that, years later, Nineveh went back to being cruel, so God finally stopped them. But Nahum also said, 'The LORD is good, a refuge in times of trouble.'"

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Act it out on a blue sheet "sea": rock the boat, splash, then everyone hides under the sheet (inside the fish) and counts "1, 2, 3 days!", then "BLEH!" and they pop out. Main idea: *God gives second chances.*
- **Explorers:** Use the "Which Way?" signs to retell the story with kids walking the route. Learn Group 2: "Jonah Met Nine Hungry fish" (Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk).
- **Trailblazers:** Read Matthew 12:38–41 and Matthew 2:1–6. Find Nineveh, Joppa and Tarshish (likely in Spain) on a map; notice how far opposite Jonah ran.
- **Ambassadors:** The book ends with a question (4:11) and no answer from Jonah. Why? Who is the "Nineveh" in your life, the person you'd rather God not bless? Discuss Nahum as the "sequel" to Jonah: God's patience has limits.

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) What swallowed Jonah? (A big fish!) (2) Did God give Jonah another chance? (Yes!) (3) Where was Jesus born? (Bethlehem!)
- *Explorers:* (1) Why did Jonah run away? (2) What did Jonah do inside the fish? (He prayed.) (3) What did the people of Nineveh do when they heard God's message?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Why was Jonah angry when God forgave Nineveh? (2) How is Jonah's "three days" like Jesus? (3) What are the three things Micah 6:8 says God requires?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Who were more obedient to God in Jonah 1, the pagan sailors or the prophet? What does that show? (2) How do Jonah and Nahum together show both God's mercy and His justice? (3) Is there a group of people you struggle to want God to love?

### Week 3: "Watch, Build & Trust" (Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah)

**Scripture:** Habakkuk 1:2; 2:1–4; 3:17–19; Zephaniah 3:17; Haggai 1:2–8, 12–14; Zechariah 4:6; 9:9

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that we can bring God our hard questions and still trust Him (Habakkuk), that God sings over His people (Zephaniah), that we should put God first (Haggai), and that the King would come riding on a donkey (Zechariah).

**Opening hook / object lesson: "The Unfinished Tower"**
- *Supplies:* A tower of blocks (or cups) only half-built, labeled "GOD'S HOUSE"; next to it, a fancy fully-built tower labeled "MY HOUSE"; a pair of binoculars (or toilet-paper-tube binoculars).
- *Do:* "Look at these two houses. One is finished and fancy. The other one, God's house, is only half done. Why do you think that is?" Let kids guess. "God's people had come home from Babylon, but they got so busy with their own houses that they stopped building God's house! God sent prophets to say, 'Put Me first!'" Hold up the binoculars: "And one prophet climbed a tower to watch for God's answer."

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> Before Babylon came, a prophet named **Habakkuk** looked around and saw lots of wrong things. People were fighting and being unfair. So Habakkuk prayed a brave, honest prayer: "How long, LORD, must I call for help, but you do not listen?"
>
> God answered, but it was a surprising answer. "I'm going to use the Babylonians to discipline my people."
>
> Habakkuk was confused. "But God, the Babylonians are even worse!" So Habakkuk said, "I will climb up my watchtower and wait to see what God will say."
>
> God answered: "Write it down. The answer will come, so wait for it." And God told Habakkuk, "The righteous person will live by his faithfulness." That means: people who belong to God keep trusting Him, even when they don't understand.
>
> And Habakkuk decided to trust. He sang, "Though the fig tree does not bud and there are no grapes on the vines… yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will be joyful in God my Savior!" Even if everything goes wrong, God is still good.
>
> Around the same time, a prophet named **Zephaniah** warned about the "Day of the LORD." But he also shared one of the sweetest promises in the Bible: "The LORD your God is with you… He will take great delight in you… he will rejoice over you with singing." Imagine God singing a happy song about you!
>
> Many years later, after God's people came home from Babylon, they started rebuilding God's temple. But then they stopped. They got busy making their own houses nice and fancy with wooden walls, while God's house stayed a pile of rubble.
>
> So God sent a prophet named **Haggai**. Haggai said, "Is it a time for you to be living in your fancy houses while God's house is a ruin? Give careful thought to your ways!" The people listened! Zerubbabel the governor, Joshua the high priest, and all the people got to work. *Bang! Bang! Bang!*
>
> God also sent a prophet named **Zechariah** to cheer them on. Zechariah had amazing visions at night. In one, he saw a golden lampstand and two olive trees. God said, "Not by might nor by power, but by my Spirit." The people didn't have to be strong; God's Spirit would help them finish! And they did. The temple was finished in 516 B.C.
>
> Zechariah also saw far into the future. He said, "Rejoice greatly! See, your king comes to you… lowly and riding on a donkey." About 500 years later, Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a donkey while the people waved palm branches and shouted "Hosanna!" The King had come!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Build a block "temple" together while you say "Bang! Bang! Build God's house!" End with a donkey ride around the room (pretend reins). Main idea: *God loves me and sings over me.*
- **Explorers:** Learn Group 3: "Zebras Hop, Zebras March" (Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi). Have kids whisper prayers of "Why?" to God and then say "Yet I will rejoice!"
- **Trailblazers:** Compare Zechariah 9:9 with Matthew 21:1–11. Read Ezra 5:1–2 to see Haggai and Zechariah in the history books. Discuss Romans 1:17 and its use of Habakkuk 2:4.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss Habakkuk as a model of honest doubt that ends in worship. Discuss Haggai's "paneled houses": what "houses" do we prioritize over God's kingdom (Matthew 6:33)?

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) What did the people stop building? (God's house!) (2) What does God do over you? (Sings!) (3) What animal did the King ride? (A donkey!)
- *Explorers:* (1) What question did Habakkuk ask God? (2) What did Haggai tell the people to do? (3) Which prophet said the King would ride a donkey?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Is it okay to ask God "why?" How did Habakkuk do it well? (2) What does "Not by might nor by power, but by my Spirit" mean for us? (3) What do Haggai and Zechariah have in common?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) How does Habakkuk 3:17–18 apply when life falls apart? (2) What would it look like to "give careful thought to your ways" with your time and money? (3) Why did Jesus choose a donkey instead of a war horse?

### Week 4: "The Messenger Is Coming" (Malachi + 🌉 The 400 Silent Years Bridge Lesson)

**Scripture:** Malachi 1:2; 3:1, 6; 4:2, 5–6; Galatians 4:4; Luke 1:5–17

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that Malachi is the last book of the Old Testament, that Malachi promised a messenger would come to get people ready for the Lord, that God was "quiet" for about 400 years but was getting the world ready, and that the silence ended when God announced John the Baptist and Jesus. Kids will celebrate knowing all 39 Old Testament books!

**Opening hook / object lesson: "The Long Wait"**
- *Supplies:* A wrapped gift box labeled "PROMISE," an hourglass or timer, a roll of blue painter's tape, 4 big signs ("PERSIA," "GREECE," "ROME," "JESUS!"), a "Shhh!" sign.
- *Do:* Hold up the gift. "I have a present for you, but you have to wait for it." Set a 60-second timer and have everyone sit **completely silent**. (Ask: "Was that hard?") "Imagine waiting not one minute, but **four hundred years**! After Malachi, God's people waited and waited for God's promise. Today we'll cross the 'Silent Bridge' and see what God was doing while they waited." Before class, tape a long "bridge" line on the floor from one side of the room to the other, with the signs spaced along it.

**📖 Story script, Part 1: Malachi (read aloud):**

> The people of Jerusalem had come home and rebuilt the temple. But after a while, they got lazy about loving God. When they brought animals to sacrifice to God, they brought the sick ones, the blind ones, the ones that limped! They kept the best for themselves. And they stopped bringing their tithe, their gift of one-tenth to God.
>
> So God sent one last Old Testament messenger. His name was **Malachi**, which means "my messenger." Malachi began with these words from God: "I have loved you."
>
> Then Malachi said, "Would you give your governor a sick, limping animal as a present? Of course not! So why would you give one to God?" He said, "Bring the whole tithe… and see if I will not throw open the floodgates of heaven."
>
> Malachi reminded them, "I the LORD do not change." God was still faithful. And then Malachi gave a promise about the future: "I will send my messenger, who will prepare the way before me. Then suddenly the Lord you are seeking will come to his temple." And at the very end of his book, Malachi said, "I will send the prophet Elijah to you…" Someone like Elijah would come first, and then the Lord Himself would come!
>
> And that is the end of the Old Testament.

**📖 Story script, Part 2: 🌉 The 400 Silent Years (walk the bridge as you tell it):**

> *(Walk to the start of the tape line with the kids. Hold up the "Shhh!" sign.)*
>
> After Malachi, something strange happened. God didn't send any more prophets. No new Bible books were written. For about **four hundred years**, it was quiet. People called it the "silent years." But God was not asleep, and God was not gone. He was busy getting the world ready for the biggest event ever!
>
> *(Walk to "PERSIA.")* First, the Persian kings ruled. God's people were back home, waiting.
>
> *(Walk to "GREECE.")* Then a young king named **Alexander the Great** marched out of Greece and conquered almost the whole world! Soon, people everywhere were speaking the same language: **Greek**. And God's Word, the Old Testament, was translated into Greek, so people from many nations could read it! (Later, the New Testament would be written in Greek too!)
>
> Then a wicked king named Antiochus tried to stop God's people from worshiping God. He messed up God's temple with a fake god's altar. But a brave family called the **Maccabees**, led by Judas "the Hammer," fought back. They cleaned up the temple and gave it back to God. Jewish families still remember this every winter at **Hanukkah**, and Jesus Himself went to the temple during this festival (John 10:22)!
>
> *(Walk to "ROME.")* Then the mighty **Roman Empire** took over. The Romans built long, straight roads everywhere, and kept the peace so people could travel safely. A king named **Herod** made the temple huge and beautiful. Everywhere, God's people gathered in synagogues to read the Scriptures and pray. And they were waiting. "When will the Messiah come? When will God speak again?"
>
> *(Walk to the end of the line, "JESUS!" Pause. Whisper:)* And then one day, an old priest named **Zechariah** was working in the temple. Suddenly an angel appeared! It was **Gabriel**, the same angel who had visited Daniel hundreds of years before! Gabriel said, "Your wife Elizabeth will have a son, and you are to call him John… He will go on before the Lord, in the spirit and power of Elijah… to make ready a people prepared for the Lord."
>
> *(Shout:)* The silence was over! Malachi's messenger was coming: John the Baptist! And just a few months later, Gabriel visited a young woman named Mary and told her she would have a baby named **JESUS**!
>
> The Bible says, "When the set time had fully come, God sent his Son." God was never late. He was getting everything ready, right on time. And now we're ready to cross the bridge into the New Testament and **meet Jesus**!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Keep the bridge walk simple: "Shhh, quiet… quiet… quiet… JESUS IS COMING!" Let them jump at the end. Main idea: *God keeps His promises. Jesus came right on time.*
- **Explorers:** Let kids carry the signs and stand at each spot. Learn to say: "39 books in the Old Testament!" Celebrate finishing the Old Testament.
- **Trailblazers:** Read Daniel 2:36–45 and match the metals to empires (gold = Babylon, silver = Medo-Persia, bronze = Greece, iron = Rome) in the traditional evangelical reading. Then read Mark 1:1–4, which quotes Malachi 3:1 and Isaiah 40:3 together.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss "the fullness of time" (Galatians 4:4): language, roads, peace, the Septuagint, synagogues and longing. Ambassadors can lead the bridge walk for Little Lambs.

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) Who is the last Old Testament book? (Malachi!) (2) Was God asleep during the quiet years? (No! He was getting ready!) (3) Who came at the end of the bridge? (Jesus!)
- *Explorers:* (1) What does the name Malachi mean? ("My messenger.") (2) What language did lots of people speak because of Alexander the Great? (Greek.) (3) Who was the messenger who got people ready for Jesus? (John the Baptist.)
- *Trailblazers:* (1) What were the people doing wrong in Malachi's day? (2) Name three ways God got the world ready for Jesus during the 400 years. (3) Which angel broke the silence, and where else does he appear in the Bible?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Why might God have waited 400 years? (2) How does Malachi 3:1 connect the two Testaments? (3) What does it mean for your life that God is never late, even when He seems silent?

### Cumulative recall drill (5 minutes)

1. **Three Groups of Four:** Split the room into three teams. Team 1 shouts Hosea–Obadiah, Team 2 shouts Jonah–Habakkuk, Team 3 shouts Zephaniah–Malachi. Rotate.
2. **Silly Sentences:** "Happy Jellyfish Ate Oranges / Jonah Met Nine Hungry fish / Zebras Hop, Zebras March." Teacher says the sentence word, kids say the book.
3. **Section Stand-Up:** Teacher calls a section (Law, History, Poetry, Major, Minor); kids stand and say that section's books.
4. **Before & After (39 books):** "What's after Daniel?" (Hosea) "What's before Malachi?" (Zechariah) "What's after Obadiah?" (Jonah) "What's the last book of the Old Testament?" (Malachi)
5. **39-Book Countdown (Trailblazers+):** Say all 39 books while holding up fingers in groups of ten (four hands = 40, minus one!). Time it and beat your record.

### Tips for each age group
- **Little Lambs:** Focus on "Minor Prophets," "Jonah," "Malachi = last," and the motions. Celebrate "39!" as a big number.
- **Explorers:** Goal: say all 12 in order (using the three groups) and spell at least 8.
- **Trailblazers:** Goal: say all 39 Old Testament books in order without the song; one-word summary for each Minor Prophet.
- **Ambassadors:** Goal: say all 39; teach the three-groups trick to a younger class; explain the 400 Silent Years in 1 minute.

## Games

### 🎮 Game #1: Minor Prophet Line-Up Race
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers, Ambassadors (Little Lambs with buddies)
- **Supplies:** Two sets of 12 cards, each with a Minor Prophet name (and picture symbol for younger kids)
- **Setup:** Two teams. Shuffle each set and give one card to each player (if a team has fewer than 12 players, some get two).
- **How to play:** On "GO!", each team must line up in correct Bible order, holding their cards at chest level, and then shout the books in order with motions.
- **How to win:** First team in correct order wins the round. Play best of 3, reshuffling each time.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Use only Group 1 (4 cards) or Group 2. *Older:* Add the 5 Major Prophets (17 cards) or do it silently (no talking!).
- **Learning goal:** Book order for all 12 Minor Prophets.

### 🎮 Game #2: Jonah Overboard!
- **Best for:** All ages (very active)
- **Supplies:** A big blue sheet or tarp (the sea), a hula hoop or taped square (the boat), a beach ball (the big fish)
- **Setup:** Lay the blue sheet on the floor around the "boat." Everyone starts in the boat.
- **How to play:** The teacher is the "Storm." Call out commands: "Wind blows: rock left!" "Waves crash: crouch down!" "Throw cargo: toss pretend boxes!" "Sailors pray: fold hands!" Then the teacher asks a trivia question to one player. A wrong answer means "Jonah overboard!": that player jumps into the "sea." Players in the sea can get back in the boat when the "big fish" (beach ball) is rolled to them and they answer a question correctly ("GULP… BLEH!").
- **How to win:** Players still in the boat at the end, or the whole class "gets to Nineveh" if everyone ends in the boat.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* No one stays out; just do the motions and splash. *Older:* Use harder trivia and "Before & After" book questions.
- **Learning goal:** Jonah's story; God's second chances; trivia review.

### 🎮 Game #3: Temple Builders Relay
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Supplies:** Cardboard boxes, cups or large blocks (about 12 per team), each labeled with a Minor Prophet (or all 39 OT books for older kids)
- **Setup:** Blocks are piled at the far end of the room. Teams line up at the start.
- **How to play:** One runner at a time runs to the pile, finds the next book in order, and brings it back. The team stacks them in order to build a "temple wall" (Hosea at the bottom, Malachi at the top). If a block is out of order, Haggai (the teacher) calls "Give careful thought to your ways!" and the team must fix it.
- **How to win:** First team to build a 12-block tower in correct order that stands for 5 seconds wins.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Blocks have pictures; just stack them, and the teacher helps with order. *Older:* Build all 39 OT books in order, sorted by section.
- **Learning goal:** Book order; Haggai's message to put God first.

### 🎮 Game #4: Cross the Silent Bridge
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Supplies:** Painter's tape "bridge" (two parallel lines about 2 feet apart across the room), signs: MALACHI (start), PERSIA, GREECE, ROME, JESUS! (finish)
- **Setup:** Kids line up at MALACHI. Teacher stands at JESUS!
- **How to play:** It's a silent game! Kids must cross the bridge without making a sound (no talking, no giggling!). At each sign, the teacher holds up a card with a question (shown, not said aloud, or acted out); kids answer by doing a motion or pointing (e.g., "Show me the Jonah motion!" "Hold up fingers: how many Minor Prophets?"). Anyone who makes a sound goes back one sign. At the end, everyone who reaches "JESUS!" can finally shout as loud as they want: "JESUS IS COMING!"
- **How to win:** Everyone who crosses wins. The class wins together when everyone reaches the end.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Allow whispers; teacher helps. *Older:* Questions are about the 400 years ("Show me with fingers about how many years," "Point to the empire that spread Greek").
- **Learning goal:** The 400 Silent Years; the waiting and the joy of Jesus' coming.

### 🎮 Game #5: Old Testament Library Sort
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers, Ambassadors (review Sunday)
- **Supplies:** 39 cards (one per OT book) per team; 5 colored "shelf" mats or baskets labeled Law, History, Poetry/Wisdom, Major Prophets, Minor Prophets
- **Setup:** Shuffle the cards and place them in a pile in the center for each team.
- **How to play:** Teams race to sort all 39 books onto the correct shelves, *and in correct order* on each shelf.
- **How to win:** First team with all 39 correctly sorted and ordered. (Ambassadors can be the "librarians" who check.)
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Little Lambs sort only the Minor Prophets with picture cards, or match pictures to "Jonah" and "Malachi." *Older:* No section labels; they must name the sections themselves.
- **Learning goal:** Mastery of all 39 Old Testament books and their sections.

### 🎮 Game #6 (bonus): Locust Swarm Tag
- **Best for:** Little Lambs, Explorers
- **Supplies:** Green scarves or pinnies for "locusts"; paper "crops" (green paper leaves) scattered on the floor
- **How to play:** Locusts (2–3 kids) try to tag the "farmers." Farmers protect the crops by standing on them. When tagged, a farmer must "rend your heart" (put a hand on their heart) and say "Return to the LORD!", then they're free again. After 2 minutes, count how many crops are left.
- **Learning goal:** Joel's message to turn back to God; God restores "what the locusts have eaten" (Joel 2:25).

## Activities & Crafts

### ❤️ Activity #1: Never-Quitting Heart Chain & Plumb Line (Week 1)
- **Supplies:** Red and pink paper strips (1" × 8"), glue sticks or tape, markers; string, metal washers, craft sticks.
- **Steps (Heart Chain):**
  1. On each strip write one way God shows love (or one way kids can show love to others).
  2. Loop and glue the strips into a paper chain. Talk about how a chain holds on, like God's love.
  3. Add a heart tag that says "God's love won't quit!" (Hosea).
- **Steps (Plumb Line):** Tie a washer to a 12" string, then tie the other end to a craft stick labeled "AMOS: Let justice roll!" Let kids test whether things in the room are straight.
- **Age adaptations:** *Little Lambs:* Pre-cut and pre-written strips; they glue. *Explorers:* Write their own. *Trailblazers:* Write a different Minor Prophet's name on each link (in order!). *Ambassadors:* Write "justice" action ideas (help a classmate, give to a food drive) on the plumb line tag.

### 🐋 Activity #2: Paper-Bag Big Fish (Week 2)
- **Supplies:** Brown or white paper lunch bags, newspaper or tissue for stuffing, blue/gray paint or crayons, googly eyes, rubber bands, a small paper "Jonah" figure (print or draw).

### 🧠 Big Questions for Ambassadors (apologetics / worldview)

**1. "Isn't Jonah just a fish tale, a myth?"**
*Suggested answer:* Jonah son of Amittai is a historical prophet mentioned in 2 Kings 14:25. Nineveh was a real, very large city (its ruins are near modern Mosul, Iraq). Jesus spoke of Jonah's experience and Nineveh's repentance as real events, setting them beside His own resurrection and the coming judgment (Matthew 12:39–41). If God can raise Jesus from the dead, He can certainly preserve Jonah in a fish. The question really comes down to whether miracles are possible, and the resurrection is the test case.

**2. "If God is loving, why does Nahum celebrate the destruction of a city?"**
*Suggested answer:* Assyria was infamous for brutal cruelty to conquered peoples. God had shown mercy to Nineveh through Jonah about a century earlier, and they returned to violence. For the many nations Assyria had terrorized, Nineveh's fall was a rescue. Nahum 1:3 holds both truths: "The LORD is slow to anger but great in power; the LORD will not leave the guilty unpunished." A God who never judged evil would not be good. At the cross, God's justice and mercy meet: Jesus takes the judgment so the guilty who repent can be forgiven.

**3. "Why should we trust that the Old Testament ended with Malachi? What about the books from the 400 years?"**
*Suggested answer:* Jewish tradition itself recognized that prophecy had ceased after the prophets of the Persian period (for example, 1 Maccabees 9:27 speaks of a time when prophets had ceased to appear, and the historian Josephus wrote similarly). Jesus referred to the Scriptures as "the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms" (Luke 24:44), matching the Hebrew Bible's three-part arrangement, which contains the same content as the Protestant 39-book Old Testament. The Apocrypha can be valuable history, but it doesn't claim the prophetic authority of "Thus says the LORD." The next prophet, John the Baptist, was announced by an angel, exactly as Malachi promised.
