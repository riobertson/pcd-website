## The Big Idea (in kid words)

Remember the long, quiet bridge of 400 years? Now we've crossed it, and **Jesus is here!** The word **Gospel** means **"Good News."** Four writers, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, each wrote a true book about Jesus' life. Jesus taught about God, healed sick people, calmed a storm, loved people nobody else loved, died on the cross for our sins, and came back to life on the third day!

**How this points to Jesus:** These books don't just point to Jesus. They show Him to us face to face! Everything the Old Testament promised comes true here: born in Bethlehem (Micah), the messenger John the Baptist (Malachi), the King on a donkey (Zechariah), the Servant pierced for our sins (Isaiah), three days like Jonah, and the Son of Man (Daniel). John tells us why the Gospels were written: "that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (John 20:31).

## The Books in Easy Words

> **Four Gospels, one Jesus.** Imagine four people watching the same soccer game from four different seats. One notices the coach, one notices the fast plays, one notices the fans, and one notices the star player's heart. They tell the same true game in four ways. That's like the four Gospels! Each one shows Jesus from a different "seat."
>
> **Teacher note:** Matthew, Mark and Luke are called the **Synoptic Gospels** ("seeing together") because they share many of the same stories in a similar order. John is different: about 90% of John's material is not found in the other three.

> **Fun teacher fact:** Since the early church, Christians have matched the Gospels with the four living creatures of Ezekiel 1 and Revelation 4 (human, lion, ox, eagle). The most common pairing (from Jerome) is Matthew = human, Mark = lion, Luke = ox, John = eagle. You'll see these symbols in old church art and stained glass. They're tradition, not Scripture, but they can be a fun "spot the symbol" activity!

## Teacher Preparation

### Background for the teacher

**What is a Gospel?** "Gospel" (Greek *euangelion*) means "good news." Each Gospel is a theological biography: a true account of Jesus' life, death and resurrection, shaped to show who Jesus is and why it matters. They aren't a complete diary (John 21:25 says the world couldn't hold all the books!); each writer selected stories for his readers.

| Gospel | Likely first readers | Emphasis | Length | Unique highlights |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Matthew** | Jewish Christians | Jesus the promised Messiah-King; fulfillment of the Old Testament; Jesus' teaching (five big teaching sections) | 28 ch. | Wise men, flight to Egypt, Sermon on the Mount (full), Peter walks on water, Great Commission |
| **Mark** | Roman / Gentile Christians | Jesus the powerful, suffering Servant; fast-paced action; discipleship includes the cross | 16 ch. (shortest) | Fast pace ("immediately"), Jesus' emotions, the centurion's confession |
| **Luke** | Theophilus and Gentile readers | Jesus the Savior of all; careful history; outsiders, women, the poor, prayer, the Holy Spirit | 24 ch. (longest book in the NT) | Birth of John, shepherds, boy Jesus, Good Samaritan, Lost Son, Zacchaeus, Emmaus |
| **John** | Christians and seekers everywhere | Jesus the eternal Son of God; believing in Him brings eternal life | 21 ch. | Seven signs, seven "I AM" sayings, Nicodemus, the woman at the well, Lazarus, foot-washing, Thomas |

**Timeline & map notes.**
- Jesus was born **about 6–4 B.C.** (before Herod the Great died in 4 B.C.; our calendar's "year 1" was miscalculated in the 500s A.D.). He grew up in **Nazareth** in Galilee, began His public ministry around age 30 (Luke 3:23), and ministered for about three years. He was crucified under **Pontius Pilate** around **A.D. 30 or 33**, and rose on the third day.
- Draw a simple map of Israel: **Galilee** (north, with the Sea of Galilee, Nazareth, Capernaum, Cana), **Samaria** (middle, where the woman at the well lived), and **Judea** (south, with Jerusalem, Bethlehem, Bethany and Jericho, Zacchaeus's town). The **Jordan River** connects the Sea of Galilee to the Dead Sea (Jesus was baptized there).
- The Gospels were likely written between about A.D. 50 and 95, within the lifetime of eyewitnesses (compare 1 Corinthians 15:3–8, which Paul wrote around A.D. 55 and which includes an even earlier creed).

**Connections to Phase 8 (the bridge).** Start Week 1 with a quick recap: Malachi promised a messenger → 400 quiet years → Gabriel visits Zechariah (Luke 1) → John the Baptist "prepares the way" (Mark 1:2–4) → Jesus! Point out that the empires from the bridge show up now: Caesar Augustus (Luke 2:1), Herod (Matthew 2), Roman soldiers and Pilate, Pharisees and Sadducees, synagogues, and the Greek language the Gospels were written in.

### Key truths to emphasize
- **Jesus is fully God and fully human.** He got tired and wept (John 4:6; 11:35), and He calmed storms and forgave sins (Mark 2:5–12; 4:39).
- **Jesus fulfilled the Old Testament promises.**
- **Jesus loves everyone**, including children, outsiders, sinners and the sick.
- **Jesus died on the cross for our sins** and **rose again on the third day.** This is the center of the gospel (1 Corinthians 15:3–4).
- **We are saved by believing in Jesus** (John 3:16; 20:31), not by being good enough.
- **Jesus calls us to follow Him** and tell others (Matthew 28:19–20).

### 🙏 Sharing the gospel and responding (important for this phase)
This phase naturally invites kids to trust Jesus. Be ready, warm and **never pressuring**. A simple "ABC" outline:
- **A**dmit: "I have sinned and need God's forgiveness" (Romans 3:23).
- **B**elieve: "Jesus is God's Son, He died for my sins, and He rose again" (John 3:16; Romans 10:9).
- **C**hoose to follow: "I want Jesus to be my Lord and Savior and to follow Him" (John 1:12).

If a child wants to talk or pray, do so in a visible, open space (follow your church's child-safety policy) with another adult nearby. Pray simply together, celebrate, and **tell the parents** (and your pastor/children's director) so they can follow up. Avoid group "raise your hand" pressure with young children; offer individual conversations instead.

### Common kid questions (with simple answers)

1. **"Was Jesus a real person?"** Yes! Four Gospels written within one lifetime of Jesus tell about Him, and even non-Christian historians from the first and early second centuries (such as the Jewish historian Josephus and the Roman historian Tacitus) mention Him.
2. **"Is Jesus God or God's Son?"** Both! Christians believe in one God in three persons: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Jesus is God the Son, who became a human baby (John 1:1, 14).
3. **"Why are there four Gospels instead of one?"** Each writer shows a different side of Jesus, like four photos of the same person from different angles. Having four witnesses also makes the story even more trustworthy (Deuteronomy 19:15).
4. **"Why did Jesus have to die?"** Sin separates us from God and deserves punishment. Jesus, who never sinned, took our punishment in our place so we could be forgiven (Isaiah 53:5; 1 Peter 3:18).
5. **"How did Jesus come back to life?"** God the Father raised Him by His power (Acts 2:24). Many people saw Him alive: the women, the disciples, Thomas, and over 500 people at once (1 Corinthians 15:6).
6. **"Where is Jesus now?"** He went back up to heaven (Luke 24:51; Acts 1:9). He is alive, He hears our prayers, and He promised to come back.
7. **"Did Jesus have brothers and sisters?"** The Gospels mention brothers, including James and Jude, and sisters (Mark 6:3). (Teachers: some Christian traditions understand these as cousins or step-siblings; keep it simple.)
8. **"Can I be Jesus' friend even though I'm a kid?"** Yes! Jesus said, "Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them" (Mark 10:14).

## Weekly Lessons

### Week 1: "Jesus Is the King!" (Matthew)

**Scripture:** Matthew 1:21–23; 2:1–12; 5:14–16; 7:24–27; 28:18–20

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that the New Testament begins with Matthew, that Matthew shows Jesus is the promised King (born in Bethlehem, worshiped by the wise men), that Jesus taught people how to live in His kingdom, and that King Jesus promises to be with us always.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "Across the Bridge"**
- *Supplies:* The floor "bridge" from Phase 8 (painter's tape), a gold paper crown, a flashlight (for the star), a small gift box with three small items (something gold, a small candle or spice for "frankincense," and a small jar for "myrrh").
- *Do:* Have kids walk silently across the bridge one more time: "Shhh… 400 years… Malachi said a messenger and the King would come…" At the far end, turn on the flashlight like a star and set the crown on a chair: "THE KING IS HERE! Welcome to the **New Testament**! The first book is Matthew, and Matthew wants you to know: Jesus is the KING!"

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> Matthew had a job that most people didn't like. He was a tax collector, and many tax collectors cheated people. But one day Jesus walked by Matthew's tax booth and said two words: "Follow me." And Matthew got up, left everything and followed Jesus! Later, Matthew wrote a book to show everyone who Jesus really is: **the King!**
>
> Matthew starts with Jesus' family tree, going all the way back to King David and to Abraham. Jesus was from the royal family, just like God promised!
>
> Then Matthew tells how an angel told Joseph, "Mary will give birth to a son, and you are to give him the name Jesus, because he will save his people from their sins." Matthew says this happened to make Isaiah's promise come true: "They will call him Immanuel," which means "God with us."
>
> When Jesus was born in Bethlehem, wise men from far away in the east saw a special star. They traveled a long way to Jerusalem and asked, "Where is the one who has been born king of the Jews? We saw his star and have come to worship him."
>
> King Herod was not happy! He didn't want another king. He asked the priests, "Where will the Messiah be born?" They said, "In Bethlehem! The prophet Micah wrote it."
>
> The star led the wise men right to the house where Jesus was. They bowed down and worshiped Him, and they gave Him treasures: gold, frankincense and myrrh, gifts fit for a king! Then God warned them in a dream not to go back to Herod, so they went home another way.
>
> When Jesus grew up, He taught people about His kingdom. He sat on a mountainside and said, "You are the light of the world… let your light shine before others." He taught them to pray, "Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name…" And He said, "Everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock." When the storm came, the house on the rock stood strong!
>
> At the very end of Matthew, after Jesus died and rose again, He gathered His disciples on a mountain and said, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Therefore go and make disciples of all nations… And surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age."
>
> Jesus is the King. He is God with us. And He is with you, always!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Give each child a turn wearing the crown and saying "Jesus is KING!" Act out the wise men following the flashlight star. Sing "The Wise Man Built His House upon the Rock" (traditional) with motions. Main idea: *Jesus is the King, and He's with me.*
- **Explorers:** Have kids find Matthew 1:1, the first page of the New Testament, and notice how many books come before it (39!). Read the Lord's Prayer together (Matthew 6:9–13).
- **Trailblazers:** Hunt for "fulfilled" verses in Matthew 1–2 (1:22–23 Isaiah; 2:5–6 Micah; 2:15 Hosea; 2:17–18 Jeremiah). Connect each one to the Phase 7 and Phase 8 prophets.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss the Great Commission (28:18–20): what does "make disciples" mean in daily life? Compare Matthew's first and last chapters ("God with us," 1:23, and "I am with you always," 28:20).

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) What did the wise men follow? (A star!) (2) What did they give Jesus? (Presents: gold!) (3) Who is the King? (Jesus!)
- *Explorers:* (1) What was Matthew's job before he followed Jesus? (Tax collector.) (2) What does "Immanuel" mean? ("God with us.") (3) What happened to the house built on the rock?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Why do you think Matthew began with a family tree? (2) Which Old Testament prophet said Jesus would be born in Bethlehem? (Micah.) (3) What does it mean to be "the light of the world"?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Why was King Herod threatened by baby Jesus? Are people still threatened by Jesus' kingship today? (2) What does it look like to build your life "on the rock"? (3) Who is one person you could help "become a disciple"?

### Week 2: "Jesus the Mighty Servant" (Mark)

**Scripture:** Mark 1:1–3; 2:1–12; 4:35–41; 10:45

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that Mark is the shortest, action-packed Gospel, that Jesus has power over sickness, sin and storms, and that Jesus, the mighty one, came to serve and to give His life for us.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "On Your Mark!"**
- *Supplies:* A stopwatch, a "START" line on the floor, a small blanket or towel (the mat), a spray bottle (storm), a fan (optional).
- *Do:* Line kids up: "On your MARK… get set… GO!" Have them race to the wall and back. "That was fast! Today's book is like that race: it's called **Mark**, and it's the fastest Gospel. Mark keeps saying 'immediately!' Jesus did this, and immediately He did that! Let's see what Jesus did in Mark."

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> Mark's book starts fast. There's no baby Jesus, no shepherds or wise men. Mark jumps right in: "The beginning of the good news about Jesus the Messiah, the Son of God!" John the Baptist is preaching by the river, Jesus is baptized, and *immediately* Jesus starts teaching, healing and helping people!
>
> One day Jesus was teaching in a house in the town of Capernaum. So many people came that the house was packed. People were squished inside and crowded outside the door. Nobody else could get in!
>
> Four friends came carrying a man on a mat. Their friend was paralyzed: he couldn't walk at all. "We have to get him to Jesus!" they said. But they couldn't get through the crowd.
>
> So what did they do? They climbed up onto the flat roof! They dug a big hole right through the roof, *scrape, scrape, scrape*, and they lowered their friend on his mat, right down in front of Jesus!
>
> Jesus saw their faith. He looked at the man and said, "Son, your sins are forgiven."
>
> Some teachers of the law thought, "What? Only God can forgive sins!" Jesus knew what they were thinking. He said, "So that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins…" He turned to the man: "Get up, take your mat and go home." And immediately the man stood up, picked up his mat and walked out in front of everyone! The people were amazed and praised God: "We have never seen anything like this!"
>
> Another day, Jesus and His disciples got into a boat to cross the Sea of Galilee. Jesus was tired, so He lay down in the back of the boat on a cushion and fell asleep. Suddenly a furious storm came up! The waves crashed over the boat, and it was filling up with water!
>
> The disciples woke Jesus. "Teacher, don't you care if we drown?"
>
> Jesus got up. He spoke to the wind and the waves: "Quiet! Be still!" And immediately, the wind stopped and everything became completely calm.
>
> The disciples were amazed. "Who is this? Even the wind and the waves obey him!"
>
> Jesus is the mighty Son of God. He can heal, forgive and calm storms. But listen to what Jesus said about why He came: "For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." The mightiest person ever came to be a servant and to give His life for us on the cross!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Act out the roof story with a towel "mat" and a teddy bear: four kids hold the corners and "lower" the bear onto a table. Then make a "storm" (wiggle, blow, sway) until you say "Quiet! Be still!" and everyone freezes. Main idea: *Jesus is strong and He helps me.*
- **Explorers:** Count how many times Mark 1 says "immediately," "at once" or "just as" (the exact number varies by translation; NIV uses several of these). Discuss: "Who are friends you could bring to Jesus?"
- **Trailblazers:** Read Mark 2:7–12. Why did Jesus forgive sins *before* healing? (He was showing He is God.) Read Mark 1:2–3 and find the Malachi and Isaiah quotes from the last two phases.
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss Mark 10:42–45: how is greatness in Jesus' kingdom different from the world's idea? Optional: discuss why many Bibles note that Mark 16:9–20 isn't in the earliest manuscripts, and how this is handled honestly by evangelical scholars (no Christian doctrine depends on those verses).

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) How did the friends get their friend to Jesus? (Through the roof!) (2) What did Jesus say to the storm? ("Quiet! Be still!") (3) Is Jesus strong? (Yes!)
- *Explorers:* (1) What did Jesus say to the paralyzed man first? ("Your sins are forgiven.") (2) What was Jesus doing when the storm started? (Sleeping!) (3) What did the disciples ask after the storm stopped?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Why were the teachers of the law upset? (2) What do the friends teach us about bringing others to Jesus? (3) What does it mean that Jesus came "to serve"?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Which storms in your life do you need to trust Jesus with? (2) How does the forgiving-then-healing order show Jesus' identity? (3) What does serving like Jesus look like at school, at home or on a team?

### Week 3: "A Savior for Everyone!" (Luke)

**Scripture:** Luke 1:1–4; 2:8–20; 19:1–10

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that Doctor Luke carefully wrote the true story of Jesus, that the angels announced Jesus as a Savior for all people, and that Jesus came "to seek and to save the lost," even people like Zacchaeus whom others didn't like.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "Lost and Found"**
- *Supplies:* A small toy sheep (or a coin) hidden somewhere in the room before class, a toy stethoscope or doctor's bag, a "step stool" or chair.
- *Do:* Put on the stethoscope: "Today's book was written by a doctor! His name is Luke. But first: oh no, I've lost my little sheep! Can you help me find it?" Let kids search. When it's found, celebrate wildly! "That's how Jesus feels about people who are lost from God. Doctor Luke tells us Jesus came to find the lost!"

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> Luke was a doctor and a careful writer. He said, "I have carefully investigated everything from the beginning," and he talked to people who had seen Jesus with their own eyes. Then he wrote it all down so we could know it's true.
>
> Luke tells us that on the night Jesus was born, some shepherds were out in the fields near Bethlehem, watching their sheep. Shepherds were poor, smelly and often ignored by important people. But God sent His angel to *them* first!
>
> Suddenly an angel appeared, and the glory of the Lord shone all around. The shepherds were terrified! But the angel said, "Do not be afraid. I bring you good news that will cause great joy for **all** the people. Today in the town of David a Savior has been born to you; he is the Messiah, the Lord." Then a huge army of angels appeared, praising God: "Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace!"
>
> The shepherds hurried to Bethlehem and found Mary, Joseph and baby Jesus, lying in a manger, just as the angel said. They told everyone what they had seen!
>
> Years later, when Jesus was grown up, He walked through the city of Jericho. A huge crowd was there. In that city lived a man named **Zacchaeus**. He was a chief tax collector, and he was very rich, because he had cheated people. Nobody liked Zacchaeus.
>
> Zacchaeus wanted to see Jesus, but he had a problem: he was short! He couldn't see over the crowd. So he ran ahead and climbed up into a sycamore-fig tree.
>
> When Jesus came to that spot, He stopped, looked up into the tree and said, "Zacchaeus, come down immediately. I must stay at your house today."
>
> Zacchaeus scrambled down and welcomed Jesus with joy! But the people grumbled, "Jesus is going to be the guest of a sinner!"
>
> But Zacchaeus's heart had changed. He stood up and said, "Look, Lord! Here and now I give half of my possessions to the poor, and if I have cheated anybody out of anything, I will pay back four times the amount."
>
> Jesus smiled and said, "Today salvation has come to this house… For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost."
>
> Shepherds or rich men, little kids or grown-ups, people everyone likes or people nobody likes: Jesus came to be the Savior for everyone. That includes you!

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Have kids "climb a tree" (stand on tiptoes, reach up), then call each child by name: "____, come down! I want to be your friend!" Main idea: *Jesus knows my name and loves me.*
- **Explorers:** Sing the traditional "Zacchaeus Was a Wee Little Man" song with motions. Find Luke 2:11 and Luke 19:10 in the Bible.
- **Trailblazers:** Read Luke 1:1–4 and discuss what makes Luke a careful historian. Compare Luke 15 (lost sheep, coin, son) with the Zacchaeus story: what's the common theme?
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss Zacchaeus's repentance: what does real change look like (making things right, generosity)? Discuss who the "Zacchaeuses" are in your school, the people others avoid.

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) Who did the angels visit? (Shepherds!) (2) Where did Zacchaeus climb? (A tree!) (3) Does Jesus love everyone? (Yes!)
- *Explorers:* (1) What was Luke's job? (Doctor.) (2) What did the angel say the good news was? (3) What did Zacchaeus promise to do after he met Jesus?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Why do you think God told shepherds first? (2) Why did people grumble about Jesus visiting Zacchaeus? (3) What does Luke 19:10 tell us about why Jesus came?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) How does Luke show special care for the poor and outsiders? (2) What does real repentance look like in Zacchaeus? (3) How can you "seek the lost" the way Jesus did?

### Week 4: "He Is Alive!" (John)

**Scripture:** John 3:16; 11:25; 19:16–30; 20:1–29; 20:31

**Lesson goal:** Kids will learn that John shows Jesus is God's Son, that Jesus died on the cross for our sins ("It is finished!") and rose again on the third day, and that John wrote so that we would **believe** and have life in Jesus' name.

**Opening hook / object lesson: "The Empty Box"**
- *Supplies:* A box or plastic egg decorated like a tomb with a "stone" lid, empty inside except for a folded strip of white cloth; a small cross; a flashlight.
- *Do:* "This box is like a tomb, a place where people were buried. What do you think is inside?" Let kids guess. Slowly open it: only a folded cloth! "It's EMPTY! Today we find out why the most important tomb in history is empty!"

**📖 Story script (read aloud):**

> John was one of Jesus' closest friends. He was a fisherman who left his nets to follow Jesus. He called himself "the disciple whom Jesus loved." Many years later, John wrote down what he had seen so that we would believe that Jesus is God's Son.
>
> John saw Jesus do amazing signs: turn water into wine, feed 5,000 people with one boy's lunch, walk on water, and open the eyes of a man born blind. John heard Jesus say, "I am the light of the world," "I am the good shepherd," and "I am the way and the truth and the life."
>
> Once, Jesus' friend Lazarus died. When Jesus arrived, Lazarus had been in the tomb for four days. Jesus told Lazarus's sister Martha, "I am the resurrection and the life." Jesus cried with His friends. Then He called out in a loud voice, "Lazarus, come out!" And Lazarus walked out of the tomb, alive!
>
> But some leaders hated Jesus. They had Him arrested. Roman soldiers made fun of Him and put a crown of thorns on His head. Then they nailed Him to a wooden cross. John was there, standing near the cross with Jesus' mother, Mary. Even while He was suffering, Jesus cared for His mom and asked John to take care of her.
>
> Then Jesus said, "It is finished." He bowed His head and died. Jesus had finished the job He came to do: paying for our sins, taking the punishment we deserved, because He loves us. His friends gently laid His body in a tomb, a cave in a garden, and a big stone was rolled in front.
>
> Everyone was so sad. But that's not the end of the story!
>
> Early on Sunday morning, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene went to the tomb. The stone had been rolled away! She ran to tell Peter and John. They ran to the tomb. John ran faster and got there first! Inside, they saw the strips of linen cloth lying there, and the cloth that had been around Jesus' head folded up by itself. The tomb was **empty**!
>
> Mary stayed outside, crying. Then she turned around and saw a man. She thought he was the gardener. Then He said her name: "Mary." She knew that voice. It was **Jesus! He was ALIVE!**
>
> Later Jesus appeared to His disciples. But Thomas wasn't there, and he said, "Unless I see the nail marks in his hands… I will not believe." A week later, Jesus came again and said, "Thomas, put your finger here; see my hands… Stop doubting and believe." Thomas fell down and said, "My Lord and my God!"
>
> John wrote: "These are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." Because "God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life." **Jesus is alive!**

**Age-group teaching tips:**
- **Little Lambs:** Use the box: "Jesus died… (close box) …but on Sunday… (open box) …He's ALIVE!" Repeat several times, and have kids shout "He's ALIVE!" Main idea: *Jesus loves me so much He died for me, and He's alive!*
- **Explorers:** List the 7 "I AM" sayings on the board and let kids draw a symbol for each. Practice John 3:16 with motions (heart, world/circle, "gave" with open hands, "believe" pointing to the head and heart, "eternal life" hands circling forever).
- **Trailblazers:** Read John 20:30–31 and discuss John's purpose. Read the Thomas story: is it okay to have questions? How did Jesus respond to Thomas's doubt (with evidence and invitation, not anger)?
- **Ambassadors:** Discuss evidence for the resurrection: the empty tomb, the many eyewitnesses (1 Cor. 15:3–8), women as the first witnesses (unlikely to be invented in that culture), and the transformed disciples who were willing to suffer. Ambassadors can lead a younger group in the "Empty Tomb Treasure Hunt."

**Discussion questions:**
- *Little Lambs:* (1) Where did Jesus die? (On a cross.) (2) What did Mary find at the tomb? (It was empty!) (3) Is Jesus alive today? (YES!)
- *Explorers:* (1) What did Jesus say before Lazarus came out? ("I am the resurrection and the life.") (2) What did Jesus mean by "It is finished"? (3) What did Thomas say when he saw Jesus?
- *Trailblazers:* (1) Why did Jesus have to die? (2) Who were the first people to see the empty tomb and the risen Jesus? (3) Why did John write his book (John 20:31)?
- *Ambassadors:* (1) Which evidence for the resurrection do you find strongest? (2) How would you explain John 3:16 to a friend who's never been to church? (3) What difference does the resurrection make for how you face death, fear or failure?

### Cumulative recall drill (5 minutes)

1. **Testament Toss:** Toss a soft ball to a child and call "Old" or "New." The child names any book from that Testament. (So far, the only New Testament books are the 4 Gospels!)
2. **Section Stand-Up:** Law → History → Poetry/Wisdom → Major → Minor → Gospels. Each group stands and says its books.
3. **Before & After across the bridge:** "What's the last book of the Old Testament?" (Malachi) "What's the first book of the New Testament?" (Matthew) "What's after Luke?" (John)
4. **Gospel Stories Match:** Teacher says a story; kids shout and do the motion of the Gospel it's (most famously) from: "Zacchaeus!" (Luke) "Wise men!" (Matthew) "Thomas sees Jesus!" (John) "Friends through the roof!" (Mark, also in Matthew and Luke!)

## Games

### 🎮 Game #1: Gospel Four Corners
- **Best for:** All ages
- **Supplies:** Four signs for the four corners of the room: MATTHEW 👑, MARK 🏃, LUKE 🤗, JOHN ❤️
- **Setup:** One child (or the teacher) is "It" and stands in the middle with eyes closed.
- **How to play:** "It" counts down from 10 while everyone runs to a corner, doing that corner's motion. At "0," "It" (eyes still closed) calls out a Gospel. Everyone in that corner must answer a question together ("Name a story from this Gospel!" or "Spell your Gospel!"). If they answer correctly, they stay in; if not, they sit out one round (or join "It" as helpers).
- **How to win:** Last players standing win. Or, for no-elimination, every correct answer earns that corner a point.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* No one sits out; they just shout the corner's name and do the motion. *Older:* Questions require a specific story unique to that Gospel ("Name a story ONLY in Luke").
- **Learning goal:** The four Gospel names, their symbols and key stories.

### 🎮 Game #2: Through the Roof Relay
- **Best for:** Explorers, Trailblazers, Ambassadors (Little Lambs with a stuffed animal)
- **Supplies:** One beach towel or small blanket per team (the mat), one stuffed animal per team (the paralyzed man), cones; optional: a table to represent the "roof"
- **Setup:** Teams of 4 (the four friends!). A finish line at the far end represents Jesus.
- **How to play:** Each team carries its "friend" on the towel (each kid holding a corner) to the far end without dropping it. At the "roof" (a table or a hula hoop on the floor), they must gently "lower" the friend down and say together, "Jesus, help our friend!" Then the friend "gets up and walks" (one teammate grabs the stuffed animal and runs it back). Then the next group of four goes.
- **How to win:** First team to have all members carry the mat wins, or the team with the fewest "drops."
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Teacher holds one corner; walk, no running. *Older:* Add a trivia question at the roof; a wrong answer means you must "dig through the roof" (10 jumping jacks) first.
- **Learning goal:** Teamwork in bringing friends to Jesus (Mark 2); Jesus' power to forgive and heal.

### 🎮 Game #3: Zacchaeus, Come Down!
- **Best for:** Little Lambs, Explorers
- **Supplies:** Chairs, low benches or carpet squares ("trees"), one fewer than the number of players (like musical chairs); music
- **Setup:** Arrange the "trees" in a circle.
- **How to play:** While the music plays, kids walk around the trees. When the music stops, everyone "climbs a tree" (stands on/at a carpet square). The child without a tree becomes "Jesus." They walk up to any tree, point and say, "______, come down! I want to come to your house today!" That child comes down, and the two link arms and help call the next round. *No one is out*: the "found" kids join Jesus' crowd, cheering. Remove a tree each round.
- **How to win:** Everyone wins when everyone has been "found"! Celebrate: "The Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost!"
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Teacher plays Jesus each round. *Older (Trailblazers):* Whoever is called down must answer a trivia question to join the crowd.
- **Learning goal:** Jesus seeks and saves the lost (Luke 19:10) and knows each of us by name.

### 🎮 Game #4: "Quiet! Be Still!" Storm Freeze
- **Best for:** All ages (great energy burner)
- **Supplies:** A blue sheet or parachute (optional), music or a rain stick
- **Setup:** Kids spread out (or hold the parachute edges).
- **How to play:** The teacher calls out storm actions: "Wind is blowing!" (sway), "Waves are crashing!" (jump), "Rain is pouring!" (wiggle fingers down), "Boat is rocking!" (rock side to side). Then, at a random moment, the teacher (as Jesus) stands up and says, "QUIET! BE STILL!" Everyone must freeze immediately and silently. Anyone who moves or giggles must answer a question ("Which Gospel are we learning?" "Spell MARK!") to rejoin.
- **How to win:** Players who freeze perfectly earn a point; the most points at the end wins (or everyone wins a "calm sea" sticker).
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Use the parachute and no questions. *Older:* Let an Ambassador be "Jesus"; add longer "freeze" times.
- **Learning goal:** Jesus' power over nature (Mark 4:35–41).

### 🎮 Game #5: Empty Tomb Treasure Hunt
- **Best for:** All ages (Ambassadors lead teams of younger kids)
- **Supplies:** 12 plastic eggs (or small boxes), each with a small item and a Scripture slip; one empty egg (the 12th); a "tomb" (cardboard box with a round "stone")
- **Setup:** Hide the eggs around the room or building. Suggested contents (in order):
  1. Tiny crown: Matthew 2:2 (wise men worship the King)
  2. Small cloth or bandage: Mark 2:11 (the man on the mat walks)
  3. Seashell or blue paper: Mark 4:39 ("Quiet! Be still!")
  4. Leaf: Luke 19:4–5 (Zacchaeus)
  5. Bread piece (cracker): John 6:35 ("I am the bread of life")
  6. Palm leaf (green paper): John 12:13 ("Hosanna!")
  7. Cup: Luke 22:20 ("the new covenant in my blood")
  8. Three coins or silver paper: Matthew 26:15 (thirty silver coins)
  9. Small cross: John 19:30 ("It is finished")
  10. Cloth strip: John 19:40 (linen strips)
  11. Small stone: John 20:1 (stone rolled away)
  12. **EMPTY!**: Matthew 28:6 ("He is not here; he has risen, just as he said.")
- **How to play:** Teams search for eggs (each team finds one color, or teams find all and then share). Then everyone sits in a circle and opens the eggs in order (numbered), reading the verse and retelling the story together. The empty egg is opened last, and everyone shouts "HE IS RISEN!"
- **How to win:** Every team that finds its eggs wins; the true "prize" is the empty egg!
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Hide in easy places; teacher reads verses. *Older:* Give riddle clues to each hiding place; teams must find the verse in their own Bibles.
- **Learning goal:** The story of Jesus' life, death and resurrection in order.

### 🎮 Game #6: Fishers of People Net Tag
- **Best for:** All ages (review Sunday)
- **Supplies:** Open space; cones for boundaries
- **Setup:** Start with two "fishermen" (Peter and Andrew!) holding hands to form a "net."
- **How to play:** Jesus said, "Come, follow me, and I will send you out to fish for people" (Mark 1:17). The fishermen chase the "fish." When they tag someone, that person joins the net by holding hands. The net keeps growing! Only the kids at the ends of the net can tag. When the net reaches 6, it can split into two nets of 3. Before joining the net, a caught fish must say one Gospel name (or the memory verse) to get "on board."
- **How to win:** The last fish caught becomes the first fisherman next round.
- **Adaptations:** *Younger:* Walk, don't run; teacher is part of the net. *Older:* Caught fish must say all 43 books before joining (or a section of them).
- **Learning goal:** Jesus calls us to follow Him and bring others; review of the Gospels.

### 🧠 Big Questions for Ambassadors (apologetics / worldview)

**1. "How do we know the Gospels are reliable and not legends?"**
*Suggested answer:* (a) **Early:** The Gospels were most likely written within 20–65 years of Jesus' death, while eyewitnesses and opponents were still alive; Paul's letters (some from the A.D. 50s) quote even earlier creeds about Jesus' death and resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:3–8). (b) **Eyewitness-based:** Luke says he investigated eyewitness testimony (Luke 1:1–4); John claims to be an eyewitness (John 21:24). (c) **Well-preserved:** We have thousands of Greek New Testament manuscripts, some fragments within about a century of writing, far more than for any other ancient work. (d) **Historically accurate details:** Places, rulers and customs (e.g., Pilate, Caiaphas, the Pool of Bethesda) have been confirmed by archaeology. (e) **Embarrassing details:** The disciples look slow and fearful, and women are the first witnesses. Inventors of a legend wouldn't have written it that way.

**2. "Didn't the Gospels contradict each other?"**
*Suggested answer:* The Gospels have differences in detail and emphasis (like how many angels were at the tomb: Matthew mentions one, Luke two). Different eyewitness accounts typically vary this way. If one mentions two angels, there was certainly at least one! Courts expect independent witnesses to agree on the core and differ on details; identical accounts would suggest copying or collusion. All four agree on the central facts: Jesus' ministry, crucifixion under Pilate, burial, empty tomb and resurrection appearances.

**3. "Did Jesus really rise from the dead, or is there another explanation?"**
*Suggested answer:* Historians, including many skeptical ones, widely accept several facts: Jesus died by crucifixion; His followers sincerely believed they saw Him alive; the persecutor Paul and the skeptic James (Jesus' brother) were converted by what they believed were appearances; and the tomb was found empty. Alternative theories struggle: hallucinations don't happen to groups of 500 at once (1 Cor. 15:6); a stolen body doesn't explain the disciples' willingness to suffer for their claim; "swoon theory" ignores Roman execution expertise. The resurrection best explains the evidence, and it is the foundation of Christian faith (1 Corinthians 15:14, 17, 20).
